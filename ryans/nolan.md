# Nolan Ryan

**Lynn Nolan Ryan Jr.** (born January 31, 1947) is an American former Major League Baseball pitcher nicknamed the "Ryan Express" [1][2]. Over a record-tying 27 seasons he pitched for the New York Mets, California Angels, Houston Astros, and Texas Rangers [1][2]. He holds the major-league career records for strikeouts (5,714) and no-hitters (seven) and was elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 1999 [1][2][3][4].

## Career
Ryan was born in Refugio, Texas, and signed with the Mets organization in 1965 [2]. He debuted in the majors in 1966 and was a member of the Mets’ 1969 World Series championship team [1][2]. Traded to the California Angels after the 1971 season, he led the American League in strikeouts in seven of his first eight Angels seasons and threw the first two of his seven no-hitters [1][2]. His 383 strikeouts in 1973 remain a modern-era single-season record [1][2].

He signed with the Houston Astros as a free agent for 1980 and later joined the Texas Rangers for 1989–93 [1][2]. He threw his sixth no-hitter in 1990 and his seventh in 1991 at age 44 [1][2]. He retired after the 1993 season with 324 wins, 292 losses, a 3.19 ERA, and 5,714 strikeouts [1][2][3]. Baseball-Reference lists him first all-time in career strikeouts, ahead of Randy Johnson (4,875) [4].

The Hall of Fame notes eight All-Star selections, 773 starts, and the lowest hits-per-nine-innings ratio (6.56) of any pitcher in history at the time of his induction [1]. Britannica records that he led his league in strikeouts 10 times in the American League alone and was the oldest pitcher to lead a league in strikeouts when he did so in 1990 at age 43 [2].

After playing, Ryan remained in baseball as an executive. Britannica reports that he became Texas Rangers team president in 2008, later CEO, resigned those roles after the 2013 season, joined the Astros as an executive adviser in 2014, and left that role after the 2019 season [2].

## Records and recognition
- Career strikeouts: 5,714 (MLB record) [1][3][4]
- Career no-hitters: 7 (MLB record) [1][2]
- Modern-era single-season strikeouts: 383 (1973) [1][2]
- Hall of Fame Class of 1999 [1][2]
