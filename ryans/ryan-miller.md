# Ryan Miller

**Ryan Miller** (also legally known as **Ryan Milton** in federal court filings) is an American tabletop and trading card game designer and brand manager at Ravensburger North America, based in the Seattle area [1][3][7][9]. With co-designer Steve Warner, he created *Disney Lorcana*, Ravensburger's first collectible trading card game, which launched in August 2023 in collaboration with The Walt Disney Company [1][2][5].

Before joining Ravensburger full-time in November 2020, Miller served as a prison guard in the United States Army at Fort Leavenworth, co-founded Sabertooth Games to publish the *Warhammer 40,000* collectible card game in 2001, and spent more than a decade at Wizards of the Coast working on *Magic: The Gathering*, *Duel Masters*, and *Kaijudo* [1][4][5][7]. Between 2013 and 2020 he worked as an independent game inventor, designing licensed tabletop titles including *Top Gun: Plot Twist*, *Aliens: Bug Hunt*, and Ravensburger's *The Princess Bride Adventure Book Game* [4][7].

## Early life and education

Miller grew up in Bakersfield, California [1]. He told the *Los Angeles Times* that his family lived "kind of on the poor side" and that his grandmother occasionally took him to Disneyland in Anaheim, where the transition from Main Street, U.S.A. into Adventureland gave him his earliest impression of environmental immersion [1][4].

He has traced his interest in game design to age 11, when a school friend described *Dungeons & Dragons* to him [3][4][7]. When his mother forbade him from playing *Dungeons & Dragons* on religious grounds, Miller took dice from the family board game collection, a pencil, and a notebook and began inventing his own games [3][7]. He told *Checkpoint Gaming* that he still kept a notebook from age 13 or 14 containing a homemade "Starfighter Combat" game [4]. When *Magic: The Gathering* debuted in 1993, Miller began playing with *Unlimited Edition* cards in 1994 and cut 3-by-5 index cards in half to prototype a two-player sword-dueling card game with his brother [1][5]. He later cited that prototype—which stalled because blocking attacks was too easy and matches never progressed—as his first lesson in trading card game pacing [1].

In the late 1990s, Miller enlisted in the United States Army with plans to pursue a career in military police and law enforcement [1][3][4]. He was stationed as a correctional specialist and prison guard at the United States Disciplinary Barracks at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, a job he later described to the *Los Angeles Times* as "98% boring and 2% terrifying" [1][7]. Because he could not afford *Warhammer 40,000* miniatures on a soldier's salary, he filled notebooks during his time at Fort Leavenworth with *Magic* deck lists and rules for a *Warhammer 40,000* card game [1]. No college degree is reported in accessible interviews, and the education field is left empty rather than inferred [1][3][7].

## Career

### 1999–2013: Sabertooth Games and Wizards of the Coast

Upon leaving the Army, Miller moved to Seattle—near Wizards of the Coast's headquarters—to pursue game design professionally [1][3][7]. He took his first industry job judging *Magic: The Gathering* tournaments five nights a week at Wizards of the Coast's tournament center [3][4][7]. In 2001, drawing on the notebooks he had kept in the Army, Miller started Sabertooth Games and co-designed the *Warhammer 40,000 Collectible Card Game* with Luke Peterschmidt [1][4].

At Wizards of the Coast, Miller worked his way from tournament judging into retail stores and then into corporate roles as a brand manager [3][7]. Seven years after moving to Seattle, he joined Wizards of the Coast's research and development (R&D) department as a game designer, spending roughly a decade with the company overall before departing in 2013 [3][4][7]. During his tenure in R&D, he worked on *Duel Masters*—where he first collaborated with future *Disney Lorcana* co-designer Steve Warner—as well as *Magic: The Gathering* sets including *Magic 2013* (*M13*) and the first *Magic: The Gathering Commander* preconstructed decks [4][5][8].

### 2013–2020: Independent game design and licensed tabletop titles

In 2013 Miller left Wizards of the Coast to work as a solo game inventor and freelance designer [4][7]. Over the next seven years he designed and consulted on card and board games tied to entertainment properties, including *Star Wars*, *Godzilla*, a *Tokyo Ghoul* card game, *Top Gun: Plot Twist*, and *Aliens: Bug Hunt* [4][7]. He told *GamesHub* that during his consulting work he had talked several companies out of entering the trading card game market because of the difficulty of sustaining a new collectible game [6].

Miller's relationship with Ravensburger began during his freelance period when the publisher contacted him to pitch a board game adaptation of the 1987 film *The Princess Bride* [4][7]. Miller adapted a "game in a book" concept he had been developing for two years—originally conceived as a haunted-house book where each two-page spread represented a room—into *The Princess Bride Adventure Book Game* [7]. Working with Ravensburger producer Steve Warner, whom he had known for more than 20 years, Miller structured the cooperative game around six chapters of a physical board-book representing scenes from the movie, with players working together to advance the plot before the framing story's grandson interrupted his grandfather's reading [7][8].

### 2020–present: Ravensburger and *Disney Lorcana*

In November 2020 Miller joined Ravensburger North America full-time to co-design a new Disney trading card game, initially codenamed "Project Famous," alongside Steve Warner [5][6][7]. Miller told *GamesHub* that although he was initially skeptical about launching a new trading card game, Ravensburger's 60-year licensing relationship with Disney and its work on the *Disney Villainous* board game line convinced him that the company had the institutional backing to support a long-term game [1][6].

Because of the COVID-19 pandemic, Miller and Warner developed the core rules online from their respective homes over roughly six months between November 2020 and mid-2021 [1][2][5][8]. They tested and discarded at least a dozen prototype systems, including a procedural "engine builder" card row and a strict heroes-versus-villains system in which heroes could only challenge villains [1][5][6][8]. Both designers concluded that mechanical novelty for its own sake distracted from the Disney characters and stories, opting instead for a streamlined ruleset that fit on the front and back of a single sheet of paper while putting strategic depth onto the cards themselves [1][2][5][6].

Drawing on their prior work on *Duel Masters*, Miller and Warner built *Disney Lorcana* around a universal resource system called "ink," in which players place cards face-down from their hand into an inkwell once per turn rather than relying on separate land or energy cards [2][5]. To create a balancing lever for tournament play, they introduced a distinction between "inkable" and "uninkable" cards, removing the inkwell symbol from high-impact control, discard, and direct-damage cards so players had to weigh deck consistency against raw card power [2][5]. Miller also originated the game's "Song" card mechanic, which allows a character already in play of sufficient ink cost to "sing" an action card from a Disney film without the player spending ink [1][2][3].

For the first two expansions—*The First Chapter* (released at Gen Con and hobby stores on August 18, 2023, and at mass retail on September 1, 2023) and *Rise of the Floodborn* (November 2023)—Miller and Warner divided the game's six ink colors three apiece to build the initial card design files [1][2][5][8]. Miller designed the Sapphire and Amethyst pools in those early sets, including cards such as *Belle – Strange but Special*, *Gramma Tala – Storyteller*, *Tamatoa – Drab Little Crab*, and the *Merlin* and *Madam Mim* self-bounce duel cycle inspired by his favorite Disney animated film, *The Sword in the Stone* [3][4][5][8]. He and Warner also co-designed the rules for "Location" cards, which debuted in the third set, *Into the Inklands*, in March 2024 [3][5].

Around the development of the third set, Miller transitioned from day-to-day set design into the role of Brand Manager for *Disney Lorcana* at Ravensburger North America, while Warner assumed leadership of the full game design team [3][5]. As brand manager, Miller became a primary public spokesperson for the game across press tours, conventions such as D23 and Gen Con, and community play initiatives, and developed the casual "Pack Rush" sealed play format, in which two players shuffle two unopened booster packs together and race to 15 lore points using the packs' marketing insert cards as starting ink [3][4][5][8][10].

### Upper Deck litigation (2023–2025)

In June 2023, two months before *Disney Lorcana* launched, trading card publisher The Upper Deck Company sued Miller and Ravensburger in federal court, alleging that *Disney Lorcana* incorporated game design elements from an unreleased trading card game (*Shell Beach*, later retitled *Rush of Ikorr*) that Miller had worked on under a work-for-hire contract with Upper Deck prior to joining Ravensburger [1][9]. Ravensburger denied the allegations, stating that the company stood behind its team and the originality of its products [1][9].

In October 2025, a federal judge granted summary judgment dismissing all of Upper Deck's claims against Ravensburger and rejecting Upper Deck's effort to amend its complaint, leaving only a contract claim against Miller [9]. Later that month, ICv2 reported that Upper Deck settled the remaining claim against Miller through a mutually agreed court judgment of $39,000, which Ravensburger paid on Miller's behalf; Ravensburger Chief Product Officer Filip Francke stated that the figure represented the contractor fees Upper Deck had originally paid Miller and that "everyone at Ravensburger, including Ryan, acted appropriately and professionally" [9].

## Notable work and achievements

- Co-designer and brand manager of *Disney Lorcana* (Ravensburger, 2023–present), co-created with Steve Warner [1][2][5].
- Designer of *The Princess Bride Adventure Book Game* (Ravensburger, 2020) [4][7].
- Designer of licensed tabletop games including *Top Gun: Plot Twist*, *Aliens: Bug Hunt*, and card games based on *Tokyo Ghoul*, *Star Wars*, and *Godzilla* during his independent design career (2013–2020) [4][7].
- Game designer at Wizards of the Coast, contributing to *Duel Masters*, *Magic: The Gathering* (*Magic 2013* and the inaugural *Commander* decks) [4][5].
- Co-designer, with Luke Peterschmidt, of the *Warhammer 40,000 Collectible Card Game* at Sabertooth Games (2001) [1][4].

## Public profile and social media

Miller does not maintain a verified personal X account; his public commentary appears through Ravensburger press events, convention panels at Gen Con and D23, and interviews with gaming and mainstream media outlets [1][2][3][8]. In an August 2021 interview with *Mojo Nation*, Miller noted that he had performed improv comedy in Seattle for 30 years—including playing Westley in an improvised stage production of *The Princess Bride*—and credited improv training with teaching him to commit quickly to specific thematic constraints during early game prototyping [7]. On October 23, 2026, *The Vector Brief* highlighted an interview with Miller on Ravensburger's multi-year roadmap for *Disney Lorcana* and the challenge of balancing accessible entry points for families with long-term card synergies for competitive players [10].

## Personal life

In interviews with *Polygon* and *Mojo Nation*, Miller has been described as an Army veteran and long-time Seattle-area resident who performs improv comedy and sings baritone [2][3][7]. At D23 in August 2024, he referenced providing for his family while joking about nondisclosure rules around future Disney franchises [8]. No further family details were disclosed in the sources reviewed for this page.

## Recognition

- *Disney Lorcana* was described by *The Toy Book* editor-in-chief James Zahn in the *Los Angeles Times* in August 2023 as "the largest, most buzzed-about customizable trading card game launch that we've seen in several years" [1].
