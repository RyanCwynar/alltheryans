# Ryan Breslow

**Ryan Breslow** is an American entrepreneur and co-founder of **Bolt**, a checkout and payments software company for online retailers. After stepping down as CEO in early 2022 amid controversy, he was reinstated as CEO in March 2025 [1][3].

## Career

Breslow co-founded Bolt, which sells software intended to speed online checkout. The company raised large venture rounds and, in January 2022, was valued at about **$11 billion** after a financing that included investors such as BlackRock-managed funds [1][4]. TechCrunch has reported total venture funding around $1 billion at the peak era [1].

Ten days after that Series E announcement, Breslow publicly attacked Sequoia Capital and Y Combinator on Twitter; he stepped down as CEO shortly afterward [2][4]. Successors included Maju Kuruvilla and later interim CEO Justin Grooms [1][4]. Coverage of the period also included customer disputes, investor conflicts, and reports of an SEC inquiry that Forbes later described as dropped [2][3].

Forbes reported in March 2024 that a company share buyback priced equity at roughly a **97% markdown** from the peak, implying a valuation near **$300 million** [2][3]. In August 2024, reporting described a contested plan to raise about $450 million (including “marketing credits”) at a proposed $14 billion valuation tied to Breslow’s return; major investors sued, and the plan later stalled and was settled [3][4].

In March 2025, Bolt told TechCrunch that the board had unanimously reinstated Breslow as CEO, with Grooms becoming president [1]. Forbes covered subsequent lawsuit settlements and quoted Breslow acknowledging past mistakes [3].

## Notable work

- Co-founded Bolt and led it through an $11 billion peak valuation (2022) [1].
- Left and later returned as CEO (2025) after a deep valuation reset [1][2][3].

## Caution on wealth claims

Peak paper valuations briefly made Breslow’s stake look enormous in secondary coverage; that status did not survive Bolt’s later markdown. This encyclopedia does **not** treat unsourced “youngest billionaire” rankings as current fact [2][3].
