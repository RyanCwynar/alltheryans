# Ryan Kaji

**Ryan Kaji** (born 2011) is an American child entertainer and the on-camera star of the YouTube brand **Ryan’s World** (originally Ryan ToysReview), produced with his family [1][2]. Forbes ranked him the highest-paid YouTube star for three consecutive years (2018–2020) [1][2][3][4].

## YouTube career
Forbes reported that the main channel launched in March 2015, when Ryan was three, with short toy-unboxing and play videos [1]. By December 2018 Forbes estimated he earned $22 million in the prior 12 months and listed about 17.3 million followers and nearly 26 billion views on the channel group [1]. In 2019 Forbes again ranked him No. 1, estimating $26 million, and described the rebranded Ryan’s World channel as having about 23 million subscribers [2]. In December 2020 Forbes placed him first for a third year; contemporaneous Forbes coverage cited roughly $29.5–$30 million in estimated earnings and described a merchandising business sold through major retailers [3][4].

Forbes’s 2018 profile said nearly all of that year’s estimated income came from pre-roll advertising across Ryan ToysReview and related family channels, with additional deals after a partnership with kids studio pocket.watch [1]. By 2020 Forbes emphasized licensed merchandise—toys and other products sold at retailers such as Target, Amazon, and Walmart—alongside the YouTube channels as the business diversified [3][4].

Subscriber and view totals change quickly; this page does not assert a current count as of October 2026 because no fresh, dated primary figure was verified for this rewrite [1][2][3].

## Television and brand
Nickelodeon’s press site describes *Ryan’s Mystery Playdate* as a preschool series created and produced by pocket.watch, following Ryan, his parents, and animated characters Gus the Gummy Gator and Combo Panda through physical challenges and unboxing puzzles [5]. A September 24, 2021 Nickelodeon release announced additional season-four episodes of the series [5]. Forbes likewise noted the Nickelodeon series and consumer products as part of the family’s business beyond advertising revenue [3][4].
