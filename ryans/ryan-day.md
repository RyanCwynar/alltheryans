# Ryan Day

**Ryan Patrick Day** (born March 12, 1979) is an American college and professional football coach and former collegiate quarterback who serves as the head football coach at The Ohio State University [1][2][4]. Named to succeed Urban Meyer on December 4, 2018, after going 3–0 as acting head coach at the start of the 2018 season, Day officially took over as Ohio State's head coach on January 2, 2019 [1][2][3].

Through October 2026, Day has guided the Buckeyes to five College Football Playoff (CFP) appearances, two outright Big Ten Conference championships (2019 and 2020), and the 2024 College Football Playoff National Championship, capped by a 34–23 victory over Notre Dame on January 20, 2025, in the inaugural 12-team playoff format [1][2]. Entering Ohio State's October 31, 2026 game at USC, his career head-coaching record stands at 88–13 overall (82–12 entering the 2026 season, plus a 6–1 start in 2026) [1][6]. In February 2025, Ohio State announced a new seven-year contract valued at $12.5 million annually that runs through the 2031 season [2].

## Early life and education

Day was born on March 12, 1979, in Manchester, New Hampshire [1][3][4]. His father, Raymond Day, owned and operated a convenience store in the Manchester area and died by suicide in January 1988, when Ryan was nine years old and his younger brothers, Chris and Tim, were seven and five [5]. Day was raised in a single-parent household by his mother with support from his grandparents, uncles, youth coaches, and the family of his childhood friend and future wife, Christina ("Nina") Spirou, whom he met when they played on the same tee-ball team at age six [2][5].

Attending Manchester Central High School, Day played quarterback and defensive back and was named the 1996 New Hampshire Gatorade High School Player of the Year as a senior [1][3]. He was recruited to the University of New Hampshire (UNH) by head coach Bill Bowes [3]. Under head coach Sean McDonnell and offensive coordinator Chip Kelly, who became a career-long mentor, Day served as UNH's starting quarterback for three seasons (1999–2001) and was elected team captain as a senior in 2001 [1][3].

At New Hampshire, Day became the first Wildcat quarterback to attempt more than 1,000 career passes (1,089) and graduated with nine school records, including career completions (653), touchdown passes (53), completion percentage (59.9%), and total offense (8,492 yards, including 7,670 passing yards) [1][3]. On November 4, 2000, he set a still-standing UNH single-game record with 65 pass attempts while rallying the Wildcats from a 31–3 late third-quarter deficit to a 45–44 overtime win at No. 2 Delaware [1][3]. Day earned his bachelor's degree in business administration from the University of New Hampshire in 2002 and completed a master's degree in administrative studies at Boston College in 2004 [1][2][3]. In 2026 he was announced as an inductee into the UNH Athletics Hall of Fame [1].

## Career

### 2002–2014: Early college coaching at UNH, Boston College, Florida, and Temple

Day began his coaching career in 2002 at his alma mater, coaching tight ends at New Hampshire under Sean McDonnell and Chip Kelly [1][3][4]. From 2003 to 2004 he served as an offensive graduate assistant at Boston College while completing his master's degree, overlapping with Boston College offensive lineman Ryan Poles and quarterback Matt Ryan [1][4][8]. In 2005 he worked as a graduate assistant at the University of Florida under first-year head coach Urban Meyer, helping the Gators to a 9–3 season and an Outback Bowl victory over Iowa [1][4].

In 2006 Day joined Al Golden's staff at Temple University as wide receivers coach before returning to Boston College for five seasons (2007–2011) as wide receivers coach [1][3][4]. His first season back in Chestnut Hill coincided with Matt Ryan's senior campaign in 2007, when Boston College went 11–3 and won the Champs Sports Bowl, and in 2008 he served on the same Boston College staff as graduate assistant Ryan Poles [1][4][8].

In 2012 Day reunited with head coach Steve Addazio—under whom he had worked at Florida—as offensive coordinator and wide receivers coach at Temple [1][3]. When Addazio was hired as head coach at Boston College in 2013, Day followed him for a third stint at Boston College, serving as offensive coordinator and quarterbacks coach for the 2013 and 2014 seasons and helping the Eagles reach bowl games in both years [1][3][4].

### 2015–2016: NFL quarterbacks coach with Philadelphia and San Francisco

In January 2015, Chip Kelly—then head coach of the Philadelphia Eagles—hired Day for his first NFL role as Philadelphia's quarterbacks coach [1][3][4]. During the 2015 season, Eagles quarterback Sam Bradford set a franchise single-season record with a 65% completion rate and threw for a then-career-high 3,725 yards [1]. After Philadelphia dismissed Kelly late in the 2015 season and the San Francisco 49ers hired Kelly as head coach in 2016, Day followed Kelly to San Francisco as quarterbacks coach for the 2016 season, working with quarterbacks Colin Kaepernick and Blaine Gabbert [1][3][4].

### 2017–2018: Ohio State offensive coordinator and acting head coach

In January 2017, Urban Meyer hired Day at Ohio State as co-offensive coordinator and quarterbacks coach [1][3]. In his first season in Columbus, starting quarterback J.T. Barrett was named Big Ten Quarterback of the Year and set the Big Ten career record with 147 touchdowns responsible for as the Buckeyes won the Big Ten Championship Game over Wisconsin and defeated USC 24–7 in the Cotton Bowl [1].

Promoted to offensive coordinator and primary play-caller for the 2018 season, Day was named acting head coach on August 1, 2018, while Meyer served an administrative suspension covering the first three games of the season [1]. Day guided Ohio State to a 3–0 start with victories over Oregon State, Rutgers, and No. 15 TCU before Meyer returned to the sideline [1]. Across the full 2018 season, Day's offense ranked second nationally in total offense (535.6 yards per game) and passing yards (5,100), setting six Big Ten single-season team records as sophomore quarterback Dwayne Haskins threw 50 touchdown passes and became a Heisman Trophy finalist [1]. On December 4, 2018, Ohio State athletics director Gene Smith announced that Meyer would retire following the 2019 Rose Bowl and that Day would become the program's full-time head coach on January 2, 2019, under a five-year contract initially paying $4.5 million annually [1][2][3].

### 2019–2023: First five seasons as Ohio State head coach

In 2019, his first full season as head coach, Day led Ohio State to a 13–0 start and an outright Big Ten championship behind transfer quarterback Justin Fields, 2,000-yard rusher J.K. Dobbins, and defensive end Chase Young, who set a school single-season record with 16.5 sacks and joined Fields as a Heisman Trophy finalist [1]. Ohio State ranked No. 1 nationally in total defense and averaged 42.5 points per game before falling 29–23 to Clemson in the CFP Fiesta Bowl semifinal to finish 13–1 [1]. Day became the first Ohio State coach in 40 years to be named Big Ten Coach of the Year [1][2].

During the COVID-19-shortened 2020 season, Day guided Ohio State to a second consecutive outright Big Ten title (defeating Northwestern 22–10) and a 49–28 victory over Clemson in the CFP Sugar Bowl semifinal before losing 52–24 to Alabama in the CFP National Championship Game to finish 7–1 [1]. From 2021 through 2023, Ohio State recorded three consecutive 11–2 seasons [1]:

- **2021:** Behind redshirt freshman quarterback C.J. Stroud (4,435 passing yards and 44 touchdowns, fourth in Heisman voting), Ohio State led the nation in total offense and scoring offense (45.7 points per game) and defeated Utah 48–45 in the Rose Bowl [1].
- **2022:** Stroud again finished as a Heisman finalist (third) and Ohio State earned a CFP berth, losing 42–41 to eventual national champion Georgia in the Peach Bowl semifinal [1].
- **2023:** Wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. won the Biletnikoff Award and finished fourth in Heisman voting while Ohio State ranked No. 1 nationally in passing defense and finished 11–2 after a Cotton Bowl loss to Missouri [1].

### 2024–present: CFP National Championship, contract extension, and 2025–2026 seasons

In 2024 Day hired his former college mentor Chip Kelly as offensive coordinator and paired him with defensive coordinator Jim Knowles, whose unit ranked No. 1 nationally in both scoring defense and total defense [1]. After finishing the regular season 10–2, eighth-seeded Ohio State won four consecutive games over a 31-day span in the first 12-team College Football Playoff bracket: defeating No. 7 Tennessee 42–17 at Ohio Stadium on December 21, 2024; No. 1 Oregon 41–21 in the Rose Bowl quarterfinal; No. 3 Texas 28–14 in the Cotton Bowl semifinal; and No. 5 Notre Dame 34–23 on January 20, 2025, at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta to win the program's ninth national championship and first since 2014 [1][2]. The 14–2 Buckeyes became the first FBS team to defeat five top-five opponents in a single season and the first to win four CFP games in one year, making Day the fifth coach in Ohio State history—alongside Paul Brown, Woody Hayes, Jim Tressel, and Urban Meyer—to win a national title [1][2].

On February 6, 2025, Ohio State announced an agreement in principle on a new seven-year contract extending Day through the 2031 season at $12.5 million in total annual compensation (including a $2 million base salary) [2]. In the 2025 season, behind redshirt freshman quarterback Julian Sayin (fourth in Heisman voting) and a defense coordinated by Matt Patricia that allowed 9.3 points per game—the fewest by any FBS team in the CFP era—Ohio State went 12–0 in the regular season, including a road win at Michigan, before losing 13–10 to Indiana in the Big Ten Championship Game and 24–14 to Miami in the Cotton Bowl CFP quarterfinal to finish 12–2 [1]. At the 2026 NFL Draft, 11 Ohio State players were selected—most of any school, including four in the top 11 picks (Carnell Tate, Arvell Reese, Sonny Styles, and Caleb Downs)—bringing Day's tenure total to 69 NFL Draft picks and 20 first-round selections through 2026 [1].

In the 2026 season, Ohio State opened 6–1 overall and 4–0 in Big Ten play through October 17, 2026—defeating Ball State (56–3), Kent State (59–3), Illinois (42–19), No. 14 Iowa (31–14), Maryland (45–16), and Indiana (19–15), with its lone loss coming 24–23 at No. 4 Texas on September 12—entering an October 31 road matchup at USC [6].

## Notable work and achievements

- 2024 College Football Playoff National Championship head coach at Ohio State (14–2 record, four CFP victories in 31 days) [1][2].
- Two outright Big Ten Conference championships (2019 and 2020) and five CFP appearances in his first seven seasons as head coach (2019, 2020, 2022, 2024, 2025) [1].
- Coached 27 first-team All-Americans, seven Heisman Trophy finalists, and 69 NFL Draft selections (including 20 first-round picks) at Ohio State through the 2026 NFL Draft [1].
- Co-founded the Ryan and Christina Day Fund for Pediatric and Adolescent Mental Wellness at Nationwide Children's Hospital (2019) and the Nina and Ryan Day Resilience Fund at the Ohio State Wexner Medical Center (2022) [2][5].

## Public profile and social media

Day maintains a verified personal X account at `@ryandaytime`, which lists his title as "Head Football Coach of The Ohio State University" and had approximately 238,900 followers as of October 23, 2026 [7].

Drawing on his experience losing his father to suicide at age nine, Day spoke publicly about his father's death for the first time at a June 5, 2019 news conference announcing a partnership between the Day family and On Our Sleeves, a childhood mental health initiative at Nationwide Children's Hospital in Columbus [2][5]. Ryan and Nina Day pledged $100,000 in 2019 to establish the Ryan and Christina Day Fund for Pediatric and Adolescent Mental Wellness at Nationwide Children's Hospital, and in 2022 made a $1 million gift to establish the Nina and Ryan Day Resilience Fund in the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Health at The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center and College of Medicine [2][5].

## Personal life

Day married his childhood friend and high school sweetheart, Christina ("Nina") Spirou, in 2005 [2][5]. They have three children: a son, Ryan Jr. ("R.J."), and two daughters, Grace and Ourania ("Nia") [1][2].
