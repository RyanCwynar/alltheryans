# Ryan Helsley

**Ryan Dalton Helsley** (born July 18, 1994) is an American professional baseball pitcher for the Baltimore Orioles of Major League Baseball [1]. A right-handed reliever, he was the St. Louis Cardinals' closer, an All-Star in 2022, and the 2024 National League Reliever of the Year after a club-record 49 saves [1]. On October 29, 2026, he exercised a $14 million player option to stay with Baltimore for 2027, after an elbow injury cut short his first season with the team [2].

## Early life and education

Helsley attended Sequoyah High School in Tahlequah, Oklahoma, where he played baseball, basketball and football and ran track [1]. He played college baseball at Northeastern State University and was named MIAA Freshman of the Year in 2014 [1]. He is a citizen of the Cherokee Nation [1].

## Career

### St. Louis Cardinals (2019–2025)

The Cardinals drafted Helsley in the fifth round of the 2015 MLB draft, and he made his major-league debut on April 16, 2019 [1]. He broke out as the team's closer in 2022, posting a 1.25 ERA with 19 saves and 94 strikeouts in 64⅔ innings, and threw an immaculate inning against Cincinnati on September 16 of that year [1]. In 2024 he had a 2.04 ERA and 49 saves, a Cardinals single-season record, and was named NL Reliever of the Year [1]. He recorded his 100th career save on June 28, 2025 [1]. From 2022 to 2024 he saved 82 games with a combined 1.83 ERA [2].

### New York Mets (2025)

St. Louis traded Helsley to the New York Mets on July 30, 2025 [1]. He posted a 7.20 ERA in 22 games for New York [1][3].

### Baltimore Orioles (2026–present)

Helsley signed a two-year, $28 million contract with Baltimore on December 1, 2025, with the second year a player option [1][2]. He went on the injured list on July 3, 2026, with right elbow discomfort, and was shut down for the season on September 19 [1]. He finished 2026 with 17 appearances, a 4.11 ERA and 8 saves in 15⅓ innings [3]. On October 29, 2026, MLB Trade Rumors, citing the *Baltimore Banner*, reported that he would exercise the $14 million option, keeping him with the Orioles through 2027 [2][5].

## Personal life

During the 2019 National League Division Series, Helsley said the Atlanta Braves' "Tomahawk Chop" chant depicted Native Americans in a demeaning way; the Braves stopped handing out foam tomahawks and playing the chop music for Game 5 [1]. He and his wife, Alex, married in July 2021 [1].
