# Ryan Carson

**Ryan Carson** is a British-American entrepreneur known for co-founding the online coding school **Treehouse**, which he led until its December 2021 acquisition, and for earlier companies DropSend and Carsonified [1][2][3].

## Career

### DropSend and Carsonified

Carson founded **DropSend**, a web app for sending large files, and guided it to an acquisition in 2008 [2][5]. He then built **Carsonified**, which ran tech conferences and training workshops in the U.S., U.K., and Europe, featuring speakers such as Mark Zuckerberg and Evan Williams; he says the business was acquired in 2011 [2][4][5].

### Treehouse

With his wife Gillian Carson, he founded what became Treehouse in 2011, evolving from Think Vitamin Membership video tutorials into a branded online school for web design and development [1][4]. The company raised venture funding (Carson’s site cites about $23 million raised over the life of Treehouse), moved headquarters to Portland, Oregon, in 2012, and at peak employed more than 100 people [1][2][4]. Carson has written that Treehouse educated more than one million students [1][2].

In 2021, after a planned acquisition fell through, Treehouse cut staff sharply. Carson’s own chronology says the company then accepted an unsolicited offer from **Xenon Partners** and completed a sale in December 2021, ending his tenure as CEO [1]. Treehouse’s blog later confirmed the Dec. 14, 2021 acquisition and introduced new CEO Jason Gilmore, stating the platform would continue operating [3].

### After Treehouse

Carson’s site lists a 2024 founding of **Untangle**, described as AI software for divorce attorneys / family-law practices [5]. He continues to publish founder writing at ryancarson.com [1][5].

## Notable work

- DropSend (acquired 2008) [2][5].
- Carsonified conferences (acquired 2011) [2][5].
- Treehouse (2011–2021; sold to Xenon Partners) [1][3].

## Public profile

Carson posts on X as @ryancarson. Oregon Business has covered his Portland-era Treehouse leadership and workplace experiments [4].
