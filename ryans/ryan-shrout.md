# Ryan Shrout

**Ryan Shrout** is an American technology analyst and executive. He is the president and general manager of Signal65, a firm that provides performance testing and technical-marketing services, a role he took up when the company launched in December 2023 [1][3][17]. He founded the hardware-review site PC Perspective, which he owned and led until 2018, and later spent about five years at Intel, including as chief performance strategist and in client graphics and AI marketing and strategy roles [4][5][8][9].

## Early life and education

No birth date or birthplace was found in the sources opened for this page. In an October 24, 2018, farewell post on PC Perspective, Shrout wrote that he had started a website dedicated to the first AMD Athlon motherboard, K7M.com, as a high school senior in 1999 [4]. In a June 26, 2013, first-person piece for ASUS's Edge Up, he wrote that he had been writing component reviews since January 2000 and that in high school he had worked at a local PC shop repairing and building computers [13].

PC Perspective's about page, and a March 11, 2004, post by Shrout announcing the new site, describe those earlier projects as Amdmb.com and Athlonmb.com, with a start in 1999 [5][6]. In the 2004 post he wrote that Amdmb.com was his full-time work besides attending the University of Kentucky, and that he planned to graduate that May [6]. His Shrout Research and Futurum biographies state that he holds a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from the University of Kentucky [2][10]. As of November 25, 2026, his X profile and public LinkedIn profile listed Union, Kentucky [14][16].

## Career

### 1999–2018: Early sites and PC Perspective

Shrout launched PC Perspective on March 11, 2004, describing it as a broader hardware-review publication that would expand beyond the AMD-focused audience of Amdmb.com [6]. The site's about page says it was founded in 2004 after previously existing as Amdmb.com and Athlonmb.com from 1999, and lists Shrout as founder and first editor-in-chief (1999–2018) [5]. *PC Gamer* similarly reported that he officially founded the site in 2004 after running those earlier sites since 1999 [7].

In the 2018 farewell he wrote that he had written hundreds of reviews, recorded weeks of video, hosted more than 500 episodes of a weekly podcast, and co-hosted *This Week in Computer Hardware* on the TWiT network "for nearly as long" [4]. TWiT's biography page for him says he started that show on the TWiT network in 2008 and also hosted the PC Perspective Podcast [11]. The 2013 ASUS piece likewise described him as host of both shows, with Patrick Norton as his co-host on *This Week in Computer Hardware* [13]. After he accepted the Intel job, TWiT wrote on October 25, 2018, that Allyn Malventano of PC Perspective would take over co-hosting that show with Norton [12].

On October 24, 2018, Shrout announced that he had accepted a role at Intel as chief performance strategist, that his first day would be in mid to late November, and that he was relinquishing ownership of PC Perspective and closing Shrout Research [4]. *PC Gamer* reported the announcement the following day [7].

### Shrout Research

His Shrout Research team page describes him as founder and principal analyst and dates the firm to 2016–2019 and to 2023 onward [2]. In the October 2018 farewell he wrote that Shrout Research would close as he joined Intel, and that the firm had worked with large technology companies on product design, go-to-market planning, and message testing [4]. The team page says he has worked independently with product-management teams at companies including Intel, Qualcomm, AMD, NVIDIA, Arm, MediaTek, Dell, Lenovo, Huawei, HTC, Samsung, ASUS, Oculus, Microsoft, and Adobe [2]. Those company names are from his own biography of the firm and describe consulting and analyst work, not employment at those companies.

The December 2023 Signal65 launch release said he "founded Shrout Research in 2020" and had most recently been its CEO [3]. That 2020 founding date conflicts with his 2018 statement that the firm already existed and was closing, and with the date range on his own team page; this page uses the contemporaneous 2018 account and the team-page dates [2][3][4]. After leaving Intel in September 2023, he returned to the firm before Signal65 launched that December [3][8][9].

### 2018–2023: Intel

Shrout joined Intel in 2018 as chief performance strategist [4][7][8][9]. *Tom's Hardware* wrote that in that role he emphasized "real-world" usage scenarios rather than benchmarks as Intel competed with AMD, and that he later became one of the public faces of Intel's Arc graphics marketing, often appearing in videos with Intel graphics fellow Tom Petersen around the launch of the first-generation Arc Alchemist cards [8]. *HotHardware* likewise described those videos and reported his later titles as senior director of technical and competitive client marketing and, most recently, senior director of client segment strategy, CCG, in the Graphics and AI Group [9]. Signal65's about page describes the Intel tenure as five years as chief performance strategist in the competitive-analysis group and as senior director of marketing for the graphics and AI division [1].

On September 26, 2023, he posted that the previous day had been his last day at Intel [8][9]. *Tom's Hardware* and *HotHardware* reported the departure that day [8][9].

### 2023–present: Signal65

On December 7, 2023, Signal65 announced its formation, with Shrout as president and general manager [3][17]. The company's about page says Signal65 was founded in 2023 by Shrout, Patrick Moorhead, and Daniel Newman, and that it inherited the Futurum Group Labs business, previously Evaluator Group [1]. The launch release described Newman, CEO of The Futurum Group, and Moorhead, CEO of Moor Insights & Strategy, as co-founders who would serve on the board, and said the new firm merged and rebranded Futurum Labs; it described Shrout as appointed president and general manager [3]. In a LinkedIn post that day, Shrout wrote that he had been "planning and organizing" the project "with some great leaders in the space," that he would be president and GM, "joined by" Moorhead and Newman, and that he would also create a client-device segment at the company [17].

The launch release quoted him saying Signal65 could "create and lead a narrative all the way from validation and testing, to messaging and marketing, and even thought leadership and story representation" [3].

At Signal65 he has written about the firm's PINNACLE benchmark results, including GPU testing of the AMD Instinct MI355X. In a September 2026 article he reported first MI355X results in that suite, writing that, in Signal65's testing, the step from the MI300X to the MI355X was a median 2x on concurrent agents at the service level across seven models, and up to 6.5x on MiniMax-M3 [18]. In a November 2026 follow-up, bylined with a data cutoff of November 14, 2026, he wrote that Signal65 had worked with AMD to retune how MiniMax-M3 was served on the same 8-GPU MI355X node, and that in the firm's testing the node then sustained 56 concurrent agents at the service level, up from 52 [19]. Those figures are Signal65's own published test results.

## Public profile and social media

Shrout posts on X as @ryanshrout [14]. The account was created on April 23, 2007 [14]. As of November 25, 2026, his display name was "Ryan Shrout," his bio read, "Ramblings of a technology guy. President and GM at Signal65. Formerly Graphics and AI product at Intel, former owner at PC Perspective," and it listed Union, KY; the account had about 19,600 followers and was labeled verified with a blue check [14].

On November 23, 2026, quoting a Signal65 post about MiniMax-M3 software improvements on the MI355X, he wrote, "Excited to see these advancements and improvements on our AMD MI355X testing with @Signal_65 PINNACLE!" [15]. As of November 25, 2026, that post had 33 likes, 5 reposts, 1 reply, and about 1,550 views [15].

His Futurum biography describes him as a contributor to MarketWatch [10]. MarketWatch maintains an author page in his name that lists opinion pieces on chip companies [20].
