# Ryan Giggs

**Ryan Joseph Giggs** (born 29 November 1973) is a Welsh former professional footballer and coach [1][2]. He spent his entire club playing career at Manchester United (1990–2014), making 963 appearances and winning 13 Premier League titles and two UEFA Champions League titles [2][3]. He later managed the Wales national team from 2018 until resigning in June 2022 [4][5].

## Playing career
Giggs was born in Cardiff and grew up partly in Wales before his family moved to Salford [1]. He made his Manchester United debut as a substitute against Everton on 2 March 1991 [2]. Guinness World Records credited him with 963 club appearances, including a then-record 632 in the Premier League, and with 13 top-division English titles [2]. AP News, covering his retirement, likewise cited 13 league titles, two Champions Leagues, and 963 appearances [3].

He won the Champions League with United in 1998–99 and 2007–08 and made 151 appearances in the competition, a record at the time of his retirement [2]. BBC Sport summarized his United haul as 34 trophies, including four FA Cups and three League Cups [7]. Internationally he earned 64 caps and 12 goals for Wales [2][3]. He retired as a player in May 2014 and briefly served as interim player-manager, then as assistant manager under Louis van Gaal [2][3].

## Management
Giggs was appointed Wales manager in January 2018 [4][5]. He stepped away from day-to-day duties in November 2020; Robert Page led the side at UEFA Euro 2020 and through World Cup qualification in his absence [4]. On 20 June 2022 Giggs resigned with immediate effect, stating that the FAW and players should prepare for the World Cup without speculation about the head coach [4][5]. The FAW accepted the resignation and thanked him for his tenure [4].

In July 2023 BBC News reported that charges against Giggs arising from a 2020 domestic incident were withdrawn by the Crown Prosecution Service and that a judge directed not-guilty verdicts on all counts [6]. Coverage of that case is limited here to that court outcome; Giggs has not held the Wales job since 2022 [4][6].
