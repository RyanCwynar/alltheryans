# Ryan Vogel

**Ryan Vogel** is an American software engineer and founder. He is a founding developer at OpenCode, an open-source coding agent from Anomaly, and the founder of Inbound, a programmable email infrastructure service [1][2][3][5]. In October 2025, while listed as a developer advocate at Databricks, he gave a talk at Next.js Conf [10][11].

## Early life and education

No birth date or birthplace was found in the sources opened for this page. On his website he wrote that he started coding in 2015, at the age of 10, with Scratch and QBasic [1]. A résumé posted on that site lists Jacksonville, Florida, and a Bachelor of Technology at the University of North Florida, majoring in information technology and minoring in business, with an expected graduation of May 2026 [14]. The résumé appears not to have been updated after his later roles: it still lists the City of Neptune Beach job as current and does not mention Neon, Databricks, or OpenCode. As of November 25, 2026, no independent confirmation of graduation was found. His X profile listed Florida [6].

## Career

### Early work and Mandarin 3D

Vogel has written that his first tech job, in 2021, was at a shop called Tiny Little Tech Shop, where he built instant-quote software in PHP and HTML for 3D printing [1]. He later founded Mandarin 3D (also referred to on the résumé as Mandarin 3D Prints, under Exon Enterprise), a 3D-printing business built around that quoting tool [1][2][14]. The dates and sales figures he has given for the business are not consistent across sources. His website says that over the following year he "scaled it up to $70k in sales" [1]. His Cosign profile, which states that it is managed by him, repeats the $70k figure and lists the role as founder and owner from April 2022 to January 2026 [2]. The résumé lists founder and developer from October 2023 and "over $20,000 in sales in the first year" [14]. Those figures were not independently verified.

His website and Cosign profile also describe earlier work at Sensible Recycling, in electronics refurbishment and storefront operations, and at Best Buy's Geek Squad as an advanced repair agent [1][2][14]. The résumé dates the Geek Squad role from January 2023 to June 2023 [14].

### City of Neptune Beach (2023–2025)

His Cosign profile lists work for the City of Neptune Beach, Florida, from June 2023 to April 2025 [2]. On his website he wrote that he started as a contract IT worker, "became Interim CIO for the city, and finally settled as a Systems Administrator & Network Engineer before leaving" [1]. The Cosign entry uses the systems-administrator and network-engineer title, notes the earlier interim CIO period, and says he "oversaw a multi-million-dollar budget" [2]. The résumé uses only the systems-administrator title from June 2023, describes work on a Windows Active Directory network, virtualization platforms, and disaster recovery, and says he "worked with leadership to manage a multi-million dollar budget" [14]. No city records or contemporaneous news accounts confirming the interim CIO title or the budget figure were found in the sources opened for this page; those details remain his account.

### Neon and Databricks (2025)

He has written that he joined Neon, the serverless Postgres company, as a developer advocate in April 2025, and that he learned on his first day that Neon was being acquired by Databricks [1]. His site's career section and his Cosign profile list the Neon role in April–May 2025 and a subsequent Databricks role as a member of technical staff, described there as an L3 developer advocate, from May 2025 to December 2025, making tutorials, demos, and conference videos [1][2]. The official Next.js Conf 2025 schedule independently listed him as "Ryan Vogel (Developer Advocate @ Databricks)" for a 1:30–1:55 p.m. PST session on October 22, 2025, titled "Fully Integrated AI that Actually Ships" [10]. A confpad entry for the talk, with a video on YouTube, summarizes it as drawing on work on Inbound and on AI-assisted development practices [11].

### Inbound (2024–present)

Vogel founded Inbound, a programmable email service for developers, published at inbound.new [1][2][13]. His website and Cosign profile date the founding to December 2024 [1][2]. A post on his site titled "Building Inbound: Programmable Email Infrastructure" is dated January 5, 2025 [13]. The product's public GitHub repository, inboundemail/inbound, describes it as "email infrastructure for agent and indie devs"; as of November 25, 2026, that repository had 367 stars [12]. He has written that the service lets developers send and receive email, reply in existing threads, and route mail to webhooks, and that it "has grown to over 1000+ users" [1][2][13]. The user-count figure is his claim and was not independently audited for this page.

### OpenCode (2026–present)

Vogel is a founding developer at OpenCode, an open-source coding agent developed by Anomaly [1][2][3]. His website narrative and Cosign profile date the role to January 2026; the career block on his website instead lists December 2025 [1][2]. OpenCode's official `humans.txt` file, last updated November 4, 2026, lists "Ryan" with the handle x.com/ryanvogel on the product team [3]. His GitHub profile, under the login R44VC0RP, lists the company as @anomalyco and the bio "work @ opencode | building inbound.new" [5]. "Founding developer" is the title he and his Cosign profile use; OpenCode's public team list does not describe him as a company founder [2][3].

OpenCode's website, as of November 25, 2026, described the product as an open-source coding agent available as a desktop app, a terminal interface, a mobile app, and an SDK, and stated that it was "trusted by 22M+ developers" a month, with "over 220,000 GitHub stars" [4]. Those usage and star figures are the company's claims on that date. A page on Vogel's site about tools he built for the OpenCode ecosystem, including OCMT (an OpenCode-based commit-message and changelog generator) and OpenSession (a web interface for browsing OpenCode sessions), still described OpenCode as "used by over 400,000 developers monthly" [18]. An allthings.dev profile, dated as of November 2026, said he had "just soft-launched its Android app" [15]. The official OpenCode site listed a mobile app with a public TestFlight beta and a Google Play listing [4].

### dashsplat and Tesla Reality View (November 2026)

On November 21, 2026, Vogel began posting on X about a personal project to recreate Tesla's "Reality View" visualization from the dashcam cameras on his own Tesla Model 3, writing that he was doing so "for my own car before V15" [8]. Later that day he posted that a ground-layer splat had been built from the footage "in almost realtime" [19]. On November 22 he posted an "overnight update" showing "dynamic splats for moving objects like cars and busses" [7]. As of November 25, 2026, that November 22 post had about 1,450 likes and about 315,000 views [7].

The corresponding GitHub repository, R44VC0RP/dashsplat, was created on November 22, 2026. Its README describes a pipeline that calibrates the six Tesla dashcam cameras from the footage, fits Gaussian splats and a road texture, tracks other vehicles, and generates a chase view; it says the work "was built in one day and one night by a team of AI coding agents directed by Ryan Vogel, from one drive of his own Model 3 (HW4)," and that it is "an independent hobby project" not affiliated with Tesla [9]. As of November 25, 2026, the repository had 309 stars [9].

## Public profile and other work

Vogel posts on X as @ryanvogel [6]. The account was created on November 10, 2022 [6]. As of November 25, 2026, his display name was "vogel," his bio named @anomalyco and linked a YouTube channel at youtube.com/@vogeldev, listed Florida, and linked ryan.ceo; the account had about 25,700 followers and was labeled verified with a blue check [6].

His website and Cosign profile say he hosts Rebase TV, described there as an HQ Trivia-style live game show in which players answer questions in real time for cash prizes [1][2]. In an August 9, 2026, technical post he wrote, "Rebase is live trivia. I host the show, open a question, and viewers answer for real money" [17]. Buzzsprout's NextDev.fm page lists him, with Dan Goosewin, as a host of that developer podcast; episode descriptions name "Ryan Vogel and Dan Goosewin" [16]. allthings.dev recorded that he co-hosted and moderated a live NextDev.fm episode at an allthings evening on June 2, 2025 [15].
