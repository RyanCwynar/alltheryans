# Ryan Dunwoody

**Ryan Dunwoody** (born 1990 or 1991) is a British hardware engineer and entrepreneur based in Shenzhen, China [3][6]. With Jesse Lozano he co-created **pi-top**, a Raspberry Pi laptop kit that Guinness World Records lists as the first 3D-printed laptop, dated 9 November 2014 [1]. He later founded the motor-control company Motormaster and, in 2025, **Powerhouse Engineering**, a Shenzhen company that offers hardware startups engineering, prototyping and manufacturing support [6][7].

Dunwoody moved to Shenzhen in June 2015 to oversee pi-top's first production run [6]. In 2026 Powerhouse moved into a former factory floor that it runs as an office, workshop and co-working space for hardware founders, and he has said it will become a Shenzhen hardware accelerator [13][16]. Powerhouse's website says its first eight-week bootcamps launch in the first quarter of 2027 [7].

## Early life and education
Dunwoody studied engineering science at the University of Oxford [3][4]. The Asiabits podcast describes his degree as a first-class master's [6]. In November 2014, aged 23, he was described by the Telegraph and Silicon UK as an Oxford engineering science graduate [3][4]. No further details of his early life were found in accessible sources.

## Career

### 2014–2015: pi-top and the 3D-printed laptop
pi-top began in 2014 as a student project to 3D-print a laptop built around the Raspberry Pi [5]. According to pi-top's own account, Dunwoody and Lozano started it in their London flat, using a home toaster oven to solder components [2]. Dunwoody has written that the pair first built their own 3D printer to make every part of the prototype, and that he slept on a floor for five months during the build [15].

Guinness World Records lists the pi-top, created by Lozano and Dunwoody in the United Kingdom, as the first 3D-printed laptop, dated 9 November 2014 [1]. Guinness describes it as a kit of parts and design files that lets anyone with a 3D printer print a laptop case, completed with a Raspberry Pi, keyboard, display and control electronics [1]. The Telegraph reported a kit price of £180 [4]. Silicon UK reported that the Indiegogo campaign had raised about $125,000 against an $80,000 goal for the first 250 units, with the campaign still running [3]. Dunwoody has since said the campaign raised over $200,000 [15].

### 2015–2021: Shenzhen and pi-top's growth
In June 2015, aged 24, Dunwoody went to Shenzhen with no Mandarin or manufacturing experience to fulfil pi-top's crowdfunding orders [6]. The Asiabits podcast reports that he lived for five months in the factory's workers' dormitory and shipped the first production run before leaving [6]. Dunwoody has written that he shipped more than 1,000 units to backers from Shenzhen [15]. He was pi-top's co-founder and chief technology officer [8][15].

In November 2016 pi-top closed a £3.5 million ($4.3 million) Series A led by Hambro Perks, with Committed Capital participating. TechCrunch reported that this took its total raised to more than £4.9 million and that the team had grown from two to 26 in 18 months [5]. In 2018 it raised a $16 million round from Hambro Perks and Committed Capital, after about $6.5 million previously, according to VentureBeat [9]. In January 2019 TechCrunch reported that pi-top employed more than 80 people across the UK, US and China, and that its devices were with "hundreds of thousands" of schoolchildren in more than 70 countries [10]. In September 2019 the company said it had closed $4 million in new investment [11]. TechCrunch reported layoffs at pi-top in November 2019 as it pivoted toward software [12].

The Asiabits podcast reports that the Shenzhen hardware team shrank after pi-top's fourth-generation product shipped [6]. It also reports that pi-top grew to about 100 people, including 25 hardware engineers [6].

### 2021–2025: Motormaster
In 2021 Dunwoody started Motormaster, which worked on brushless motor control and battery management [6]. According to the Asiabits podcast, demand grew from startups that needed someone to develop hardware in China and manage factories [6].

### 2025–present: Powerhouse Engineering
In February 2025 Dunwoody rebranded Motormaster as Powerhouse Engineering, centred on a "fractional CTO" model for hardware startups [6]. Powerhouse describes itself as a team on the ground in Shenzhen that handles engineering, supply chain, design for manufacturing (DFM) and factory relationships for client companies, working in English and Chinese [7][8].

In July 2026 Dunwoody wrote that the company was moving to a 700 m² space [16]. Later posts describe it as a former working factory floor, now used as an office, workshop and co-working space for hardware founders, with a grand opening attended, he said, by more than 100 people [13][16]. In an August 2026 post he said Powerhouse "will become THE Shenzhen hardware accelerator," describing an eight-week program in which founders arrive with an idea and leave with a prototype, and a later funded accelerator [13]. Powerhouse's website lists the bootcamps as launching in the first quarter of 2027 [7]. As of October 2026, his X bio describes him as "launching an accelerator from an old factory" with a focus on physical AI and robotics [14].

## Notable work and achievements
- Co-creator of pi-top, listed by Guinness World Records as the first 3D-printed laptop (2014) [1].
- Founder and CEO of Powerhouse Engineering [7][8].

## Public profile and social media
Dunwoody posts on X as @Ryan__Dunwoody. In a September 2026 introduction post he said he had spent 11 years in Shenzhen, had raised $40 million, had shipped more than 500,000 units and held a Guinness World Record, and that he posts about what building hardware in Shenzhen looks like [14]. He also posts on LinkedIn, including the "Factory Walkies" and "#PowerhouseInsider" series [7][15]. The Asiabits podcast describes him as the organiser of Hardware Beers Shenzhen, and gives his Chinese name as 杜无敌 [6].
