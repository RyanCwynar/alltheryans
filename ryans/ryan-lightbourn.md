# Ryan Lightbourn

**Ryan Lightbourn** is a filmmaker whose work includes independent horror features and, later, AI-generated shorts [1][2][8]. In 2026 his 12-minute short *Black Thunder* won the Serenissima Award, for best film made with Venice, at the inaugural Lumara Film Festival, presented by MoonPay and Venice; the film was also a Grand Prize finalist [9][10][11].

## Early life and education

IMDb lists his date of birth as July 19, 1983, in Miami, Florida, and, like a Screamfest director biography that reprints much of the same text, says he was raised in Nassau, Bahamas [1][3]. A 2013 *Filmmaker Magazine* profile says he grew up in the Bahamas, and a 2017 BroadwayWorld item describes him as a Bahamian filmmaker [2][8]. As of November 16, 2026, his X profile listed his location as Rock Sound [5].

According to IMDb and the Screamfest biography, he graduated from Rollins College in 2007 and then enrolled at Full Sail University in Winter Park, Florida, first in connection with music and later in film [1][3]. *Filmmaker Magazine* says he went to business school in the United States, transferred to Full Sail after graduating, and had wanted to become a music producer before he began making short films and music videos [2]. In a 2017 interview he said filmmaking "came to me in my 20's," that he and friends entered the Apple Insomnia Film Festival after college, and that in 2008 he entered Full Sail's film program and left a few months later because he felt classroom work was taking time away from making films [4].

## Career

### Early shorts and *Roid Rage*

*Filmmaker Magazine* reported that a five-minute short titled *Sleepwalkers*, made for the Annual Zombie International Film Festival while he was in film school, won that contest, and that in 2011 his short *Roid Rage* was screened at Screamfest in Los Angeles and, as of December 2013, had more than three million views on YouTube [2]. IMDb lists *Roid Rage* among his credits, including as a segment in the 2016 anthology *Minutes Past Midnight* [1]. In the 2017 interview he said that after leaving Full Sail he began directing music videos and photographing narrative projects [4]. His IMDb biography, also carried on the Screamfest page, states that within five years he had photographed five feature films, directed two of his own, and directed more than 100 music videos; those counts were not independently confirmed for this page [1][3].

### *All the Devils Are Here* (formerly *Sleepwalkers*)

His first independent feature was shot in 2013 in Florida under the title *Sleepwalkers*, primarily on a RED Scarlet, with additional use of a Blackmagic Pocket Cinema Camera [2][4]. He told *Filmmaker Magazine* that the story follows a group of people who encounter underground demons he described as a "zombie-vampire hybrid," and that he was the director and cinematographer and was also doing the editing and much of the other production work himself [2]. In the later interview he said a rough cut premiered at the Bahamas International Film Festival, that he did not finish the film until October 2015, and that after a festival run and unsuccessful studio pitches in 2016 he released it through the self-distribution aggregator Distribber [4].

BroadwayWorld reported in October 2017 that the film, retitled *All the Devils Are Here*, had been released on digital platforms including iTunes, Amazon, Google Play, Vudu, PlayStation, and Xbox, and described it as a "synth-fueled love-letter to 80's & early 90's B-movies" [8]. Lightbourn is credited as writer and director [4][8]. In the 2017 interview he said he composed the score as a John Carpenter homage and had originally wanted a heavier use of Italo disco licensed tracks [4]. He also said that if planned follow-up features did well, he hoped to make "an ultra-violent, colorful, fantasy film called *Black Thunder*" [4].

### AI shorts and *Satoshi Sunrise*

By 2026 he was posting AI-generated shorts on a YouTube channel under his own name [6][7]. One of those uploads is titled *MoonPay Presents: Satoshi Sunrise (2026)* [7]. In April 2026 he wrote that he had pitched a proof of concept to MoonPay, and in a May 2026 reply he described the YouTube film as a 7.5-minute short [19][20]. On April 15, 2026, he wrote that *Satoshi Sunrise* was "at 6M views across platforms," a figure that was not independently audited for this page [18]. In the same post he wrote that before becoming a filmmaker he had wanted to produce music "as a side hustle" and that "Music will always be the foundation for my art" [18].

### *Black Thunder* and Lumara

In September 2026 he released *Black Thunder*, an AI-generated short, on his YouTube channel [6]. MoonPay's announcement of the Lumara awards described it as a "12-minute sci-fi fantasy epic" [11]. Latent Matinee, an AI-film journal, says the complete film was uploaded on September 18, 2026, runs about 12 minutes 23 seconds, and was also issued on the same channel as six numbered parts, plus a theme song [23].

Lumara, an AI film festival presented by MoonPay and Venice, took submissions from July 24 to September 15, 2026, and received more than 700 entries [10]. On October 16, MoonPay named *Black Thunder* one of six Grand Prize and Jury Selection finalists and a nominee for the Serenissima Award, for best film made with Venice [10][16]. Lightbourn wrote that he "made *Black Thunder* because I missed seeing movies like this on the big screen" and that it would screen at the Roxy in New York on October 26 [17]. The festival's world premiere was held that day at the Roxy Cinema in New York [9][10].

On October 21, 2026, MoonPay announced that *Black Thunder* had won the Serenissima Award, "best film made using @AskVenice," with a $12,000 prize and 1.2 million Venice credits [11]. Lumara's official site lists Lightbourn as the Serenissima winner and Jiaze Li's *Candy* as the Grand Prize winner [9]. Quoting that announcement, Lightbourn wrote that he was "incredibly grateful to Venice, Moonpay & the legenday [sic] judges" and that "My upcoming feature film, *Rainbow Sun*, will be made in @AskVenice" [12]. In later posts he described earlier shorts, including *Black Thunder* and *Rainbow Girl*, as tests for a *Rainbow Sun* universe, and on October 17, 2026, he wrote that those two titles had reached "3.5M+ views across socials," again a figure given in his own post [21][22].

### November 2026 X post

On November 13, 2026, PJ Ace (PJ Accetturo) posted an AI-made episode with the claim, "This episode cost $102,725 and took 45 days to make," and wrote that he thought his team had "finally solved AI Filmmaking" [14]. Later that day Lightbourn posted a short of his own, writing, "This episode cost $79 and took 2 days to make. I think I've finally solved AI filmmaking," and saying he would share his process and Dreamina prompts in a thread [13]. The two posts were paired in an X trending story [15]. As of November 16, 2026, Lightbourn's post had about 171,500 views, 3,396 likes, and 322 reposts [13]. The cost and schedule figures in both posts are the authors' own claims.

## Public profile and social media

Lightbourn posts on X as @ryanlightbourn [5]. The account was created on August 17, 2009. As of November 16, 2026, it had about 15,800 followers, an empty bio, a link to his Instagram, and a location listed as Rock Sound; the account was verified with X Premium [5]. He also maintains a YouTube channel at @ryanlightbourn, which hosts *Black Thunder* and *Satoshi Sunrise* [6][7].
