# Ryan Blaney

**Ryan Blaney** (born December 31, 1993) is an American stock car racing driver who drives the No. 12 Ford Mustang Dark Horse for Team Penske in the NASCAR Cup Series [1][2]. He won the NASCAR Cup Series championship in 2023 [2][5]. Team Penske lists his birthplace as Hartford, Ohio, and his residence as Mooresville, North Carolina [2].

Blaney is a third-generation driver whose father, Dave Blaney, raced in NASCAR and whose grandfather Lou Blaney raced sprint cars and modifieds [7]. He joined Team Penske's development system as an 18-year-old in 2012 [2]. In October 2026 he won the pole at Phoenix Raceway and finished second to Tyler Reddick by 0.801 seconds, leaving him 40 points behind Chase leader Kyle Larson with three races left [3][4][10].

## Early life and education

Blaney was born on December 31, 1993, in Hartford, Ohio [1][2]. His father Dave made nearly 475 Cup starts over 17 years, according to NBC Sports, and his grandfather Lou was a sprint car driver and part-owner of Sharon Speedway in Hartford, Ohio [7]. Ryan began racing quarter midgets at about age eight near Pocono while his father was racing there, and moved to late models at about 14 [7]. No college education was found in the sources reviewed for this page, so the education field is left empty rather than inferred [2].

## Career

### 2012–2015: Penske pipeline and national series

Blaney first joined Team Penske at 18, competing in select NASCAR Grand National Series races in 2012 [2]. He earned his first Grand National victory for Team Penske at Kentucky in 2013 [2]. Team Penske's biography credits him with four wins in NASCAR Truck Series competition in consecutive seasons from 2012 to 2015 [2]. He was named NASCAR Truck Series Most Popular Driver in 2014 [2].

### 2015–2017: Wood Brothers Racing

Blaney drove for Wood Brothers Racing, a Team Penske alliance partner, before moving to Penske full-time [2]. He posted his first Cup Series win in 2017 at Pocono Raceway, which was the 99th victory for Wood Brothers Racing [2][6]. NASCAR reported that he held off Kevin Harvick for that Pocono win on June 11, 2017 [6].

### 2018–2022: Team Penske No. 12

Blaney transitioned from Wood Brothers Racing to Team Penske in 2018, competing in the team's third full-season entry [2]. He won the inaugural Bank of America ROVAL 400 at Charlotte Motor Speedway in 2018 [2]. Team Penske credits him with back-to-back Talladega wins in 2019 and 2020, and with his first All-Star Race win at Texas Motor Speedway in May 2022 [2].

### 2023: Coca-Cola 600 and the championship

In May 2023 Blaney won the Coca-Cola 600 at Charlotte, leading 163 of 400 laps and ending a 59-race winless streak [2][8]. In November 2023 he won his first Cup Series championship at Phoenix Raceway, finishing second in the race to Ross Chastain but first among the Championship 4 drivers [5]. NASCAR reported that he outlasted Christopher Bell, Kyle Larson and William Byron for the title [5]. Team Penske said the title was its fourth Cup Series championship and followed Joey Logano's 2022 title [2].

### 2019–2022: Playoff streak and first multiple-win season

Team Penske credits Blaney with extending his win streak to three consecutive seasons with a Talladega victory in October 2019 [2]. It says he won again at Talladega in June 2020, becoming the first driver since Jeff Gordon in 2007 to post back-to-back victories at that track [2]. In 2021 he scored multiple Cup wins for the first time, winning at Atlanta, Michigan and Talladega, according to Team Penske [2]. In 2022 he earned his first All-Star Race win at Texas Motor Speedway in an overtime finish [2].

### 2024–2025: Championship 4 return and career highs

Blaney advanced to the Championship 4 for a second consecutive season in 2024 following a win at Martinsville Speedway in the Round of 8 finale [2]. Team Penske said he finished as Cup Series runner-up in 2024 [2]. In 2025 he set single-season career highs with four wins, 15 top-five finishes and 852 laps led, according to Team Penske [2]. Team Penske listed his 2025 wins as Nashville, Daytona, New Hampshire and Phoenix [2].

### 2026: Phoenix pole and second place

As of October 19, 2026, NASCAR lists Blaney in his 11th full-time Cup Series season, with his most recent win before the October Phoenix race at Phoenix Raceway in March 2026 [1]. On October 17, 2026, he won the Busch Light Pole Award at Phoenix with a lap of 137.143 mph (26.250 seconds) for the Freeway Insurance 500 [3]. NASCAR reported that it was his fourth pole at Phoenix, his fifth of the season and the 17th of his career [3].

On October 18, 2026, Tyler Reddick beat Blaney by 0.801 seconds after a restart on lap 294 [4]. Phoenix Raceway's report gives the same 0.801-second margin and lists Blaney second after starting first [10]. NASCAR reported that Blaney led 51 laps in six separate stints [10]. After the race, Blaney was 40 points behind Larson with three races left, and seven drivers were within reach of the championship lead [4]. The Associated Press reported that three races remained after Phoenix [11].

## Notable work and achievements

- First Cup Series win at Pocono in 2017, the 99th win for Wood Brothers Racing [2][6].
- Coca-Cola 600 winner in 2023 [2][8].
- NASCAR Cup Series champion in 2023 [2][5].
- All-Star Race winner in 2022 [2].
- Inaugural Iowa Speedway Cup race winner on June 16, 2024, which Team Penske said made him the first driver to win at Iowa in each of NASCAR's national series [2].

## Public profile and social media

Blaney posts on X as @Blaney, the handle listed by Team Penske [2]. Away from the track, Team Penske says he works with the Ryan Blaney Family Foundation on brain health causes, with a focus on Alzheimer's disease and concussions [2]. The foundation says it was founded in 2018 and that its work is rooted in the family's loss of Lou Blaney, who was diagnosed with Alzheimer's in 2001 and died in 2009 [9].

## Personal life

Team Penske reports that Blaney and his wife, Gianna, welcomed their first son, Charley, in 2025, and reside in Mooresville, North Carolina [2]. No further personal details are included, because none were verified in other accessible sources reviewed for this page [2].
