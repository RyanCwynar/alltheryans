# Ryan McKenzie

**Ryan McKenzie** is a Canadian entrepreneur and marketer. In 2019 he co-founded Tru Earth, a Vancouver-area company that sells concentrated laundry detergent strips and other household cleaning products in compact packaging, with Brad Liski and Kevin Hinton [1][2][3][4]. He served as the company's chief marketing officer until 2024 and, according to his public LinkedIn profile, remains a director [11][12][14].

## Early life

No birth date or birthplace was found in the sources opened for this page. In a July 2026 post he described himself at 45 [12]. His public LinkedIn profile lists Coquitlam, British Columbia, and as of November 20, 2026, his X profile listed Vancouver [9][14]. No educational credentials were confirmed in those sources.

## Career

### Before Tru Earth

Tru Earth's about page, in a first-person co-founder biography, says that before he co-founded the company McKenzie "had a subscription box company" [1]. His public LinkedIn profile lists an article dated October 13, 2017, titled "11 Secrets I learned while building a subscription box business from 0$ to $2,000,000 a year in 365 days"; the revenue figure in that title was not independently verified [14].

In an April 2026 post he wrote that he had been "building internet businesses since before the Dot-com bubble," that he built humor and video sites before YouTube existed, ran "half a million emails off a home server in 2000," "ranked #1 on Google for 'ringtones,'" and "grew a content site to 50M monthly visitors" before that business "collapse[d] overnight when the algorithm changed" [11]. His LinkedIn profile lists InfoBarrel.com as a project beginning in November 2005, described there as a crowdsourced online publication [14]. Those early traffic, ranking, and collapse claims were not independently confirmed for this page.

### Founding Tru Earth (2019)

McKenzie has said the company launched in April 2019 [8][16]. *Tri-City News* reported that Liski, McKenzie, and Hinton—described there as former colleagues in the media industry—licensed laundry-strip technology from a manufacturer in New Brunswick and began selling the strips through an online subscription service, starting from the corner of a warehouse in New Westminster, British Columbia [4]. *The Globe and Mail* reported that an extended family member of Liski's had invested in a product whose owners were struggling to bring it to market, that Liski, McKenzie, and Hinton tried the product themselves, and that the three "worked out an agreement with the patent holder for exclusivity" [2]. A 2026 *BC Business* profile of Liski similarly says a relative approached him in 2019 about a struggling business that held a patent for water-activated laundry strips, and that he co-founded Tru Earth that year with McKenzie and Hinton [3].

Tru Earth's about page says McKenzie called Liski, that Liski, McKenzie, and McKenzie's childhood friend Kevin each tried the strips, that Kevin set up a small webpage, and that McKenzie wrote about his experience with the product on that page [1][20]. The company, *The Globe and Mail*, *BC Business*, and The Challenger Project all report that the founders set a threshold of 150 subscription orders in the first 30 days and instead received 1,500 [1][2][3][8]. The company's about page says that within 12 months it served 60,000 people in 40 countries [1].

A 2020 Tru Earth press release said Liski was chief executive officer, McKenzie "oversees customer development and marketing," and Hinton "has built and manages the IT platform that is core to the company's customer interactions" [5]. On the June 16, 2021, episode of the E-commerce Evolution podcast, host Brett Curry introduced him as co-founder and CMO [17]. He also appeared on Perpetual Traffic in June 2020 and on the Ecommerce Impact Podcast, where he discussed video ads and other acquisition channels [16][19].

### Growth, press figures, and financing

In an October 15, 2020, press release, Tru Earth said *Canadian Business* had ranked it No. 2 on that year's STARTUP LIST, which the release described as a ranking of Canadian new-growth companies on two-year revenue growth, and that the company had recorded two-year revenue growth of 8,408 percent, "from $0 to a current run rate of over $30 Million," with "175,000 returning subscribers and purchasers" [5]. The Challenger Project later wrote that by the end of the following year Tru Earth was the second-fastest-growing start-up in Canada and, by the end of 2021, the 71st fastest-growing company in the country [8].

In a September 28, 2021, *Globe and Mail* profile of the company, Samantha Edwards wrote that Tru Earth was 71st on the newspaper's Top Growing Companies list in 2021, its second year on the list; that in the previous fiscal year "the company's revenue was $36-million and this fiscal year, they're on track to hit $55-million"; that it had grown from seven employees to 230; that the detergent strips were produced in Moncton, New Brunswick, and sent to distribution centres in British Columbia, Ontario, Washington, Tennessee, and Utah; and that it had customers in 65 countries, with its biggest markets in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia [2]. A November 24, 2021, Tru Earth press release said the company had been named Top BC Exporter for 2021 and BC Exporter of the Year in the Emerging Exports category, and that it then exported to 67 countries, with more than half of revenue coming from exports [18]. *Tri-City News* reported in 2023 that the company had moved to a 12,000-square-foot location on St. Johns Street in Port Moody that included a retail storefront, and that it also had facilities in Burnaby, Mississauga, Ontario, an office in Michigan, and another in the Netherlands [4].

On April 25, 2022, Tru Earth announced that Renewal Funds had "led an investment financing" in the company, with Export Development Canada also participating; the release did not state a dollar amount [6]. In a November 2024 interview with CEO Brad Liski, Callaway Climate Insights described the 2022 round as a "small, 'low eight figure' Series A" with those investors, and described Tru Earth as "a C$100 million laundry detergent company" [7]. On the Ecommerce Impact Podcast, McKenzie said the company had grown "completely bootstrapped to mid eight figures in revenue before we eventually did a raise earlier in 2022," and, after correcting himself, that they "were at an eight figure runway run rate within, I think about eight months" [16]. Those timing and scale figures are his account on the podcast.

Later figures given for Tru Earth's size are not consistent with one another and have not been independently audited for this page. As of November 20, 2026, McKenzie's X bio read, "Co-founder Tru Earth. $100M+ Sales. Mid 8-figure DTC native brand in less than 3 years" [9]. In the April 2026 post he wrote, "I co-founded Tru Earth and scaled it to over $150M in revenue," and that "that one sentence drove over $150M in revenue," referring to a tagline he said he had written [11]. His website, as of the same date, described him as "Co-founder of Tru Earth" and claimed "$250M+ in Online Sales," a career-sales figure that the site does not break down by company [15]. A November 16, 2026, *BC Business* article on Liski said that seven years after founding, "Tru Earth now sees eight-figure annual revenue and has expanded to over 80 countries," and that it is a Certified B Corporation [3].

### Departure from the CMO role

McKenzie's public LinkedIn profile lists him as co-founder and CMO from April 2019 to May 2024, and as a director from April 2019 onward [14]. In a May 23, 2026, post he wrote that he had "stepped down from my CMO role at Tru Earth" "about a year and a half ago" and had since been working without a team or budget-approval process [13]. In the July 2026 post he again listed "Stepped down as CMO of Tru Earth" [12]. In the April 2026 post he wrote that he had "stepped down from my CMO position to help ecommerce founders scale faster than I did" [11].

His website offers one-to-one coaching, strategic advisory, and other services aimed at ecommerce brands, and describes him as helping brands "scale from 7 to 8+ figures" [15].

## Public profile and social media

McKenzie posts on X as @RyeMcKenzie [9][16]. The account was created on May 7, 2009 [9]. As of November 20, 2026, his display name was "Ryan McKenzie | Co-Founder Tru Earth," his bio made the $100M+ sales and mid-eight-figure claims quoted above, listed a Vancouver location, and linked to a newsletter at ryanmckenzie.com; the account had about 28,400 followers and was labeled verified with a blue check [9]. A pinned-post identifier returned by the X API for that profile was not found when retrieved [9].

On November 18, 2026, quoting a product announcement from TxtCart, he wrote that "one of the biggest mistakes I see ecommerce brands make is obsessing over getting more traffic before figuring out how to monetize the traffic they already have," adding that better opt-in rates mean "more customers you can reach without paying Meta again" [10]. As of November 20, 2026, that post had about 24 likes and about 640 views [10].
