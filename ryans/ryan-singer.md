# Ryan Singer

**Ryan Singer** is an American product designer, strategist, and author. He worked at 37signals, later known for the project-management product Basecamp, from 2003 until 2021, finishing as head of strategy [1][2][5][8]. In 2019 he wrote *Shape Up: Stop Running in Circles and Ship Work that Matters*, a book about that company's product-development process [3][4]. Since leaving the company he has worked independently with product and engineering teams, a practice he and others have associated with Felt Presence [1][15][18][19].

## Early life

In a January 2015 interview Singer said he was born in Kenosha, Wisconsin, in 1983 and that he had moved to Chicago about 11 years earlier [14]. In that interview he described himself as an interface designer and product manager at a software company headquartered in Chicago [14]. No educational credentials were found in the sources opened for this page. On his website and in later biographical notes he has described himself as a self-taught web designer when he joined 37signals [17].

## Career

### 2003–2021: 37signals and Basecamp

Singer joined 37signals in 2003, when the Chicago firm was still primarily a web design shop [5][8]. In a July 2019 REWORK interview he said he had been a fan of the company's usability-focused site, applied after Jason Fried announced an opening, and was hired after a final-round exercise in which candidates were asked how they would redesign Verizon's website [5]. *The Verge* later reported that the company consisted of four people when he joined [8]. Singer has written that he was on the three-person team at 37signals that created Basecamp [1][17].

In the same REWORK interview he said that after David Heinemeier Hansson began programming Basecamp in the Ruby on Rails framework, he taught himself enough programming to implement designs directly, and that as the company hired more people he ended up in the middle between design and programming [5]. He has written that his role evolved from UI designer to product manager to head of strategy, and that he worked "on all levels of the software stack, from defining strategy to shaping projects to designing interfaces to writing code to marketing" [1][2][17]. *The Verge* described the head-of-strategy role as essentially Basecamp's chief product officer [8].

### Shape Up (2019)

On July 10, 2019, Fried announced *Shape Up: Stop Running in Circles and Ship Work that Matters* on the company's Signal v. Noise blog, crediting Singer as author and presenting the book as a free web publication at basecamp.com/shapeup [3][4]. Fried wrote that the book explained, "in replicable detail," how Basecamp did product development, including an approach developed "in isolation over nearly 15 years" that did not use backlogs, daily stand-ups, or velocity tracking [4].

The book describes a process built around six-week cycles, "shaping" work before it is scheduled, "bets" instead of backlogs, and "appetites" instead of estimates [3][4][5]. In the REWORK interview Singer said the book grew out of more than 15 years of making software at the company [5]. An about-the-author page published with the book said he had worked at Basecamp for "over 17 years" and was then focused on product strategy [2]. On his website he later wrote that he wrote the book "to formalize how we did product development over my 17 years at 37signals" [1].

Singer discussed the book on other programs around the same time, including Changelog Interviews #357, recorded on August 14, 2019, in which he said he had been with the company for 16 years [7]. In a REWORK episode dated November 17, 2020, he discussed a print edition of *Shape Up*, which the episode described as having been released around that time after he added sections in response to reader questions [6].

### Departure from Basecamp (2021)

On April 26, 2021, Fried published "Changes at Basecamp," announcing, among other internal changes, that "societal and political discussions" would no longer be allowed on the company's internal Basecamp account [9]. *The Verge* reported that after an all-hands meeting at the end of that month, Fried told staff that Singer had been suspended pending an investigation following remarks Singer made on the call, and that Singer resigned over the following weekend [8]. In an email to *The Verge*, Singer wrote: "I objected to an employee's statement that we live in a white supremacist culture. White supremacism exists, and America's history of racism still presents terrible problems, but I don't agree that we should label our entire culture with this ideology." He added that "tensions were so high after the call that I decided it won't be tenable to stay on the team" and that he "gave my resignation over the weekend" [8]. Fried told *The Verge* that Singer had resigned and said, "I denounce white supremacy unconditionally" [8]. *The Verge* also reported that, within a few hours of the meeting, at least 20 people — more than one-third of Basecamp's 57 employees — had said they intended to accept buyouts [8].

### Independent work (2021–present)

On his website Singer writes that since 2021 he has been "helping product and engineering teams get unstuck and start shipping the right things" [1][17]. In a February 2025 interview with *The American Surveyor* he said that in 2021 he "struck out on my own," helping engineering teams apply what he had learned at 37signals, and that feltpresence.com had been a personal site and domain he already had; he added, "Probably the real brand is just Ryan Singer" [19]. A Business of Software speaker biography says he founded Felt Presence in 2022 "to help product teams stop running in circles and regain the thrill of building" [15]. The same site, under the Felt Presence name, is published at ryansinger.co and feltpresence.com [1]. In a Churn.fm interview, whose discussion of plans for 2024 places it around late 2023, he was introduced as the founder of Felt Presence and discussed the work of adapting Shape Up to teams outside Basecamp [18].

His current site describes client work that includes driving product improvements "from concept to release," three-hour shaping sessions with teams adopting a Shape Up-style workflow, and live whiteboard sessions on interface problems [1]. He first taught a cohort-based course called Shaping in Real Life; a July 16, 2024, post announced a sixth cohort for October 7–22, 2024, limited to 20 spots, and said earlier cohorts had sold out in under two weeks [16]. The site later said he was sharing that material more widely as articles and videos [1]. He has also spoken at Business of Software, including talks listed there as "Debugging the Product Development Process" (March 2023) and "Framing and Hard Conversations" (March 2025) [15].

## Public profile and social media

Singer posts on X as @rjs [10]. As of November 25, 2026, the account's bio read "Product & process. Creator of Shape Up. Prev: 37signals," listed Lisbon, linked to ryansinger.co, and had about 53,200 followers [10]. The account was created on November 8, 2007 [10].

Recent posts have focused on product design and on using software agents in design and research. On November 20, 2026, he wrote about using Claude as a "turbo-boost for R&D" on breadboarding tools, and, separately, that "We need the next iteration of UML for the agent age" [12][13]. On November 23, 2026, he posted that "Good design is a compounding process of repeatedly choosing to lean on existing core elements," a post that had 95 likes as of November 25, 2026 [11]. His site lists the email address rjs@ryansinger.co and a LinkedIn profile under the handle feltpresence [1][20]. In the Churn.fm interview he mentioned that he had moved to Portugal [18].
