# Ryan Guzman

**Ryan Anthony Guzman** (born September 21, 1987) is an American actor and former model [17]. He is best known for playing Sean Asa, the male lead in the dance films *Step Up Revolution* (2012) and *Step Up: All In* (2014) [6]. He also played Noah Sandborn opposite Jennifer Lopez in the thriller *The Boy Next Door* (2015) [6][8], and since 2018 he has played firefighter Edmundo "Eddie" Diaz on the first-responder drama *9-1-1* [12][14].

Before acting, Guzman pitched in high school and college baseball, competed in mixed martial arts, and worked as a print and commercial model [3][5][6]. He had no formal dance or acting training when he was cast in *Step Up Revolution*, his feature debut [1][2]. Other credits include the ABC Family series *Pretty Little Liars*, the NBC event series *Heroes Reborn*, the musical *Jem and the Holograms*, and Richard Linklater's *Everybody Wants Some!!* [5][7][9][10]. *9-1-1* moved from Fox to ABC for its seventh season in 2024, and Guzman stayed in the main cast [15][16].

## Early life and education
Guzman was born in Abilene, Texas [1][17]. He told *Sactown Magazine* that his father was in the Air Force and that the family moved to Sacramento, California, when he was about three [3]. Sacramento was his mother's hometown, and most of his father's family had also settled there after coming from Mexico [3]. *Empire* lists his parents as Lisa (née Hudson) and Ray Guzman and says he has a younger brother, Steven [22]. NBC Latino reported that his father was born and raised in Mexico [2]. Guzman grew up in Sacramento's Rosemont area, where he attended Sequoia Elementary School and Einstein Middle School [3].

He was a pitcher at West Campus High School and then at Sierra College [3]. He told *Variety* he went 9–1 in his final season at Sierra [5]. A torn labrum and arm surgery that didn't fix it ended his baseball career [5][6]. He later called the injury "a huge setback" after devoting "13 or 14 years" to the sport [3].

## Career

### 2009–2011: Mixed martial arts and modeling
After baseball, Guzman turned to mixed martial arts and fought in welterweight competition [5][6]. He told *Variety* that he lost a welterweight match for a Northern California championship, and that the loss "made me realize I had to work harder" [5]. The *Los Angeles Times* reported that after a "disheartening loss" he decided his future lay elsewhere [6].

He also modeled. In 2010 *Sactown Magazine* described the 23-year-old Guzman as a model with Wilhelmina and Look Model Agency, appearing that season in Abercrombie & Fitch's *A&F Quarterly*, shot by Bruce Weber [4]. Rotten Tomatoes says he modeled Calvin Klein underwear in 2009 [17]. Guzman later said modeling "was never for me," calling it "very vain," and that his choice was "either try acting or go back and fight" [6].

### 2010–2014: Commercials and *Step Up*
Guzman moved to Los Angeles in 2010 and started with commercials [6]. He told *Variety* he "got six in six months" before landing *Step Up Revolution* [5]. NBC Latino reported that he heard about the film through a friend who was auditioning, and asked his manager, whom he had signed with three months earlier, to get him in [2]. He told the *Boston Herald* the casting took "four acting and six dancing auditions" [1].

In *Step Up Revolution*, the fourth film in the series, Guzman played Sean Asa, a Miami street dancer, opposite Kathryn McCormick [1][2]. NBC Latino reported that he trained for three weeks, nine hours a day, before filming [2]. Guzman told *Variety* that a dance double was brought in, but "it is me up there dancing" [5]. The *Los Angeles Times* reported that the film grossed $140 million worldwide [6]. He returned as Sean Asa in *Step Up: All In*, released in August 2014 [6].

In 2013 he joined *Pretty Little Liars* as Jake, a martial-arts instructor and love interest for Lucy Hale's character, Aria [7]. He told ET Online the role was first planned for "an episode or two," but the producers "slowly started writing me into the show more" [7]. He said fans of Aria's existing relationship with another character pushed back online when he joined, and that he had "been Tweeted some crazy, crazy things" [7]. Deadline later listed *Pretty Little Liars* among his television credits as a recurring role [9]. The *Los Angeles Times* reported that in 2013 he also wrote, directed and acted in a short film, *Deceit* [6].

### 2015–2017: Film and *Heroes Reborn*
In January 2015 Guzman starred opposite Jennifer Lopez in *The Boy Next Door*, as Noah, a young neighbor of Lopez's character [6]. According to Box Office Mojo, the film opened to $14.9 million in North America on a reported $4 million budget, and grossed about $35.4 million domestically and $52.4 million worldwide [8].

In March 2015 Deadline reported that Guzman had joined NBC's 13-episode event series *Heroes Reborn*, a follow-up to *Heroes* from creator Tim Kring [9]. That year he also appeared in the musical *Jem and the Holograms*. He told the *Los Angeles Times* that singing for the film was "the first time I've sung in front of people" [6][5].

Guzman played Kenny Roper, one of the team captains and "a very big party guy," in Richard Linklater's *Everybody Wants Some!!*, an ensemble comedy about a college baseball team in the 1980s [3]. Linklater called the film a "spiritual sequel" to his earlier film *Dazed and Confused* [3][10][23]. The cast also included Blake Jenner, Glen Powell, Zoey Deutch, Tyler Hoechlin and Wyatt Russell [10][23]. Guzman told *Variety* that the role let him "relive my baseball career," and that the cast nicknamed Linklater "Rickapedia" because "he's so knowledgeable" [5]. IndieWire reported that the film would open the 2016 SXSW Film Festival on March 11, 2016, ahead of a Paramount release on April 15, 2016 [10]. Guzman told *Sactown Magazine* that the cast trained at the University of Texas baseball complex. He described the film as "*Animal House* meets *Bull Durham*" [3].

### 2018–present: *9-1-1*
In 2018 Guzman had a small role in *Papi Chulo*, which *The Hollywood Reporter* reviewed out of the Toronto International Film Festival [11]. On May 23, 2018, Fox announced that he would join *9-1-1*, created by Ryan Murphy, Brad Falchuk and Tim Minear, as a series regular for its second season [12][13]. *The Hollywood Reporter* noted that the drama had become Fox's top series after its early-2018 launch [13]. Executive producer Tim Minear told *Entertainment Weekly* that Guzman's character, Eddie Diaz, was an Army veteran and a single father to a son with cerebral palsy [14]. Eddie's son, Christopher, is played by Gavin McHugh [18]. Asked by TV Insider about portraying a parent of a child with cerebral palsy, Guzman said the point was that Christopher "doesn't let that affect them in the way that we would think that it should," and that "it's not stopping him from doing the day-to-day tasks" [18]. In the same 2018 interview, given while he was expecting his first child, he said his first scene with McHugh made him think about "the day that I get to walk my own son to school" [18]. *The Hollywood Reporter* reported at the time of his casting that *9-1-1* averaged 14.9 million viewers per episode once all platforms were counted [13].

In May 2023 Fox declined to renew *9-1-1* after six seasons, and the series moved to ABC, the sister network of its studio, 20th Television [15]. Guzman was named in the returning cast [15]. The seventh season premiered on ABC on March 14, 2024, and the show's 100th episode was scheduled for April 4, 2024 [16].

## Notable work and achievements
- Lead role in *Step Up Revolution* (2012), which the *Los Angeles Times* reported grossed $140 million worldwide [6], and its sequel *Step Up: All In* (2014) [6].
- Co-lead opposite Jennifer Lopez in *The Boy Next Door* (2015), which grossed about $52.4 million worldwide on a reported $4 million budget [8].
- Ensemble role in Richard Linklater's *Everybody Wants Some!!*, the opening-night film of SXSW 2016 [10].
- Series regular on *9-1-1* since season two (2018), across its moves from Fox to ABC [12][15][16].

## Public profile and social media
Guzman posts on Instagram as @ryanaguzman [19]. He has talked about his heritage in interviews. In 2012 he told NBC Latino he wanted to "help out my culture and progress" [2]. In 2014 he told the *Los Angeles Times* that he was glad his *Step Up* role was not a stereotyped Latino character [6].

In June 2020, in an Instagram Live video, Guzman defended his then-fiancée's use of a racial slur in tweets from 2011, and said that he and his friends used slurs with each other [19][20]. *Variety* reported that castmates Oliver Stark and Aisha Hinds publicly criticized his comments [20]. On June 3, 2020, he posted an apology on Instagram, writing: "I am truly sorry, hold myself accountable and take full responsibility for my defensiveness and ignorance" [19].

## Personal life
In January 2019, Guzman and his then-fiancée, actress Chrysti Ane, had a son, Mateo [21].
