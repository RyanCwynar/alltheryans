# Ryan Petersen

**Ryan Petersen** (born 1980 or 1981) is an American businessman and venture capitalist. He founded and runs **Flexport**, a technology-focused freight forwarder and customs broker based in San Francisco [1][4]. Earlier in his career he worked in his family's import-export business, and in 2007 he co-founded the shipping-data company **ImportGenius** [1]. Since 2023 he has also been a partner at the venture capital firm Founders Fund [3].

Petersen started Flexport in 2013, and it went through the Y Combinator accelerator in 2014 [1]. By February 2022 the company had raised nearly $2.3 billion and was valued at $8 billion [4]. He gave the chief executive role to former Amazon executive Dave Clark in 2022–23, then took it back in September 2023 when Clark resigned [5][10]. In October 2021, a Twitter thread he wrote about congestion at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach was widely shared and was followed by a change to local container-stacking rules [13][14].

## Early life and education
Petersen grew up in Bethesda, Maryland [1][2]. According to Forbes, his mother was a biochemist who ran a business helping companies manage food-safety regulations. His father was a government economist who analyzed Soviet defense spending and ran a coding business on the side [2].

As a 17-year-old high school senior, Petersen started buying goods from China with his older brother David, who was then studying computer science at the University of Maryland. They resold the goods on eBay; Bloomberg lists knives, scooters and all-terrain vehicles [1]. Their mother lent them $15,000 to start, and Petersen told Bloomberg that at one point they were moving millions of dollars of merchandise a month [1]. David and a friend, Michael Kanko, turned the venture into a company called KPX [1].

Petersen graduated from the University of California, Berkeley, with a degree in economics in 2002 [1][2]. Forbes reported that he first tried to build a career in global microlending, without success [2]. He later earned an MBA at Columbia University [1]. Bloomberg reported in 2015 that he spoke Mandarin, Spanish, Portuguese and French in addition to English [1].

## Career

### 2002–2007: KPX and China
Petersen joined KPX in 2002 and moved to China to be closer to its suppliers [1]. He told Bloomberg he lived in Shanghai and Kunming for two years, and that the experience left him frustrated with what he saw as the hassle of traditional freight forwarding [1]. Besides importing, KPX's founders built about 40 single-brand e-commerce sites selling products mostly made in China. One of them, Wasauna, became a large U.S. importer of walk-in bathtubs [1].

### 2007–2013: ImportGenius
In 2007, while Petersen was at Columbia, he, his brother David and Michael Kanko founded ImportGenius [1]. The service sells access to a database of historical shipping manifests and tracks shipments entering the United States and other countries [1]. As of 2015, Bloomberg reported that it charged $200 a month, had about 4,500 subscribers and held 380 million historical manifests. Its clients included investment banks, hedge funds and large logistics firms [1].

### 2013–2019: Founding and early growth of Flexport
Forbes reported that Petersen had long pursued the idea of a "TurboTax for customs paperwork." He needed a background check to become a licensed customs broker, which was approved in March 2013, and then pitched Flexport to Y Combinator co-founder Paul Graham [2]. Flexport graduated from Y Combinator in 2014 [1]. In 2015, Bloomberg described it as "the world's first licensed customs brokerage built around a modern Web application." At the time it had raised $6.6 million, from investors including Google Ventures and Ashton Kutcher's fund, and had 31 employees [1]. David Petersen left to co-found BuildZoom [1].

Founders Fund led a $20 million Series A in 2015 [2]. In September 2017, Forbes reported that Flexport was closing a $110 million Series C at a $910 million post-money valuation, bringing its total funding to $204 million [7]. In February 2019 it raised $1 billion in a round led by the SoftBank Vision Fund, at a reported post-money valuation of $3.2 billion [15]. Petersen told Forbes at the time that Flexport had 1,066 employees in 11 offices and four warehouses [15].

### 2020–2022: Pandemic growth and the Series E
CNBC reported that during the COVID-19 pandemic, Flexport moved tens of millions of masks around the world [4]. In February 2022 the company announced a $935 million Series E that valued it at $8 billion. Andreessen Horowitz and Michael Dell's MSD Partners led the round, and Shopify made a strategic investment [4]. Existing investors DST Global, Founders Fund and SoftBank's Vision Fund I also took part. That brought total funding to nearly $2.3 billion [4]. At the time, Flexport said it had more than 10,000 clients and suppliers in 112 countries [4].

### 2022–2023: CEO transition
In June 2022, Flexport announced that Dave Clark, then Amazon's worldwide consumer chief, would become its CEO on September 1, 2022, and that Petersen would become executive chairman in March 2023 [5]. Petersen and Clark served as co-CEOs from September 2022, and Clark became sole CEO in March 2023 [10].

In January 2023 the co-CEOs announced that Flexport would cut about 20% of its global workforce, roughly 640 employees, citing the macroeconomic downturn and lower shipping volumes [6][18]. In May 2023 Flexport agreed to acquire most of Shopify's logistics business, including Deliverr, in an all-stock deal. The deal closed on June 6, 2023, and gave Shopify a 13% equity stake on top of its existing holding [8][9].

On September 6, 2023, Clark announced his resignation. He wrote that Petersen wanted "to return to focusing on growth in the core freight business," and Petersen became CEO again [10]. Two days later, Petersen said on X that Flexport would rescind offers to about 75 people who had been due to join, and cancel more than 200 open job postings [11]. He wrote: "I am deeply sorry to those people who were expecting to join our company and won't be able to at this time" [11]. TechCrunch reported that after his return, Petersen criticized his predecessor for overspending on hiring and expansion, and oversaw cost cuts that included laying off about 20% of staff, around 600 people [12].

### 2023: Convoy acquisition
In November 2023, Flexport acquired the assets of Convoy, a digital freight startup that had shut down. Petersen said in a staff memo that Flexport would not take on the business or its liabilities, but would keep a small product and engineering team [12]. Convoy had been valued at $3.8 billion in April 2022 [12]. The terms were not disclosed. Petersen wrote that "the purchase price relative to value is modest," and said Flexport planned to offer a full range of trucking services to its forwarding customers [12].

### 2024–present: Automation and AI
In September 2026, Flexport announced a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server that lets customers' AI agents work with its platform, along with upgrades to its "Atlas" world model [17]. The company also announced its first fully automated e-commerce fulfillment center, outside Chicago. Flexport said the site has more than 350 robots that bring inventory to workers, and that it runs at double the efficiency of a traditional warehouse [16][17]. Flexport lists the Chicago warehouse in Franklin Park, Illinois, and also describes a robotic facility in Pleasant Prairie, Wisconsin [16]. On October 12, 2026, Petersen introduced the Chicago site on X with the post "Warehouse robots are here!" [19].

## Notable work and achievements
- Co-founded ImportGenius, a subscription database of shipping manifests (2007) [1].
- Founded Flexport (2013), which reached an $8 billion valuation in 2022 [4].
- Led Flexport's purchase of Convoy's technology (2023) [12]. The acquisition of Shopify Logistics (2023) took place while Clark was CEO [8].
- Has made about 150 personal angel investments, according to Forbes, including Rippling, Anduril and Carta [3].

### Investing
In July 2023 Forbes reported that Petersen was joining Founders Fund as a partner, investing as a generalist from its early-stage and growth funds [3]. Founders Fund had invested in every Flexport round up to that point and led three of them [3]. Petersen told Forbes: "I'm going to be a bit of a rookie here, trying to learn from all of them" [3].

## Public profile and social media
Petersen posts on X as @typesfast, where he has more than 750,000 followers as of October 2026. His bio reads "Founder and CEO of Flexport. Tariff whisperer" [19].

In October 2021 he rented a boat and toured the Port of Long Beach complex with the head of one of Flexport's partners. He then posted a thread about the container backlog, and it went viral [13]. The Los Angeles Times reported that the thread included five recommendations, among them lifting limits on how high containers could be stacked [14]. California Governor Gavin Newsom contacted him to discuss it [14]. Long Beach temporarily raised its stacking limit from two containers to four by the end of that day [14]. The Times also reported that the change's impact was "difficult to measure," and that the head of the Harbor Trucking Association said it did not change much [14]. Forbes noted criticism from logistics insiders [2].

Petersen appeared on the cover of Forbes in February 2022 [3].
