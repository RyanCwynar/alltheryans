# Ryan Peterman

**Ryan Peterman** is a software engineer, writer and podcaster. He worked at Instagram, part of Meta, for about seven years and was promoted to Staff software engineer three years out of college [1][2][6]. He writes the newsletter *The Peterman Post*, which his site lists at more than 100,000 subscribers, and hosts *The Peterman Pod*, an interview podcast about the careers of technical people [1][12].

In February 2026 Peterman announced that he had left Meta to work full time on the podcast and on Compose, an ultra-low-profile ergonomic keyboard he is designing [6][7]. A Kickstarter campaign for Compose raised $85,625 against a $35,000 goal from 410 backers between March 26 and April 25, 2026 [9]. His December 2025 interview with Boris Cherny, the creator of Anthropic's Claude Code, was among the episodes he named when describing the podcast's costs [6][11].

## Early life and education

Peterman wrote that his high school had no programming classes and that he did not know what computer science was until he took UCLA's introductory course CS31 [3]. In a 2023 newsletter post he wrote that only 3 of the 56 classes he took at UCLA mattered in his daily engineering work, naming CS31, CS32 and the lab course CS35L [3]. ## Career

### Amazon

After graduating, Peterman worked at Amazon in a small satellite office [2][6]. He wrote that he had no mentorship there [2]. His newsletter's About page puts his time at Amazon at eight months, while his 2026 resignation post describes it as "a few months" [2][6].

### 2018–2026: Instagram and Meta

Peterman then joined Instagram [2]. In a June 2024 post he wrote that six years earlier he had asked to join the Instagram Media Infrastructure team even though he was more junior than the hiring manager wanted, and that he stayed on that team for six years [5]. He has written that he was promoted from new-grad engineer (IC3) to Staff engineer (IC6) in three years [2].

His published engineering work at Meta focused on video infrastructure [1]. With Haixia Shi, he co-authored a November 2022 Meta Engineering post describing how Instagram reduced the compute cost of producing basic adaptive-bit-rate video encodings by 94 percent [4]. The approach repackaged existing progressive encodings into adaptive-bit-rate formats, and the post said the savings let Instagram increase watch-time coverage of advanced encodings by 33 percent [4]. The post noted that Instagram had more than 2 billion monthly active users at the time [4]. Peterman's personal site says Mark Zuckerberg publicly discussed part of the work behind his Staff promotion [1]. His site also lists earlier engineering posts on video processing latency (2020), Threads send latency and Instagram video ads performance (2021) [1].

In June 2024 Peterman wrote that he had transferred from Instagram Media Infrastructure to an AI/ML training infrastructure team, citing growth in the AI field and the high share of senior engineers in the new organization [5]. In his February 2026 resignation post he wrote that a reorganization "at Zuck's level" had moved him into an organization building ML infrastructure, where he was a manager, and that he chose to leave before building out a new team [6].

### 2024–present: The Peterman Pod

Peterman wrote in February 2026 that he had started the podcast about a year earlier [6]. Apple Podcasts lists the show's years active as 2024 to 2026 and, as of October 26, 2026, lists 73 episodes and a weekly update schedule [12]. The show describes itself as "Sharing the transparent career stories of technical people" [12].

At the time he quit, he wrote that YouTube had paid him $14,994.63 while he had spent $17,600 on video editing, plus about $8,000 on in-person episodes such as the Boris Cherny interview [6]. In a September 2026 update, he wrote that he went four months into a planned six-month runway without income, then signed sponsors including Cursor and WorkOS and began recording episodes himself to cut costs [8]. He wrote that nine months after leaving, the podcast was sustaining itself [8].

Guests named in his own posts and episode listings include Boris Cherny, Bjarne Stroustrup, Brian Kernighan, Paul Mockapetris, Scott Aaronson, Casey Muratori, Philip Su, Ethan Evans and Thariq Shihipar [8][11][12]. He wrote that his interview with Stroustrup, the creator of C++, was shot in Stroustrup's study with two iPhones [8].

### 2026–present: Compose

Peterman wrote that he had used ergonomic keyboards for about five years because of wrist pain and could not find one that matched what he wanted [6]. His site describes Compose as an "ultra-low profile keyboard" [1]. The Kickstarter campaign ran for 30 days from March 26 to April 25, 2026, and closed with $85,625 pledged from 410 backers against a $35,000 goal [9]. Peterman later wrote that the campaign raised the funds needed within a day of launch [8].

On September 16, 2026, he reported receiving the first sample from the assembly line and said he had used it as his only keyboard for two weeks [10]. He wrote that it was wireless, slimmer than his previous Kinesis keyboard and used low-travel scissor switches [10]. The same post said the next batch would be about 60 sets, and that a delayed circuit-board assembly component could push the schedule back by up to four weeks in the worst case [10]. In his September 18 update he wrote that the keyboard was on track to ship early the following year [8].

## Notable work and achievements

- Co-authored Meta Engineering's 2022 account of a 94 percent reduction in Instagram's basic video encoding compute [4].
- Promoted from IC3 to Staff (IC6) at Instagram within three years, by his own account [2].
- Grew *The Peterman Post* newsletter to more than 100,000 subscribers, according to his site [1].
- Hosts *The Peterman Pod*, with 73 episodes listed on Apple Podcasts as of October 26, 2026 [12].
- Raised $85,625 from 410 Kickstarter backers for the Compose keyboard in 2026 [9].

## Public profile and social media

Peterman posts on X as @ryanlpeterman [13]. As of October 26, 2026, his X bio read "Quit my job to build the podcast & ergonomic keyboard I wish existed • ex-software engineer @instagram, @meta" and listed his location as "nyc / sf" [13]. The account had about 30,100 followers at that time [13]. His pinned post, from February 3, 2026, announced that he had "quit the best job I ever had" to work on the podcast and Compose [7].

His interview with Boris Cherny was published December 15, 2025 [11]. The episode runs about 1 hour 24 minutes and covers Cherny's career path to creating Claude Code [11]. Excerpts from the episode continued to circulate on X in October 2026, including an October 24 summary thread by the account @RealAlphaApp [14].
